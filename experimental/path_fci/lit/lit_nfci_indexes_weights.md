# Literature notes: NFCI/ANFCI, stress indexes, GS FCI weights, funds-rate equivalents
Compiled 22 Oct 2026. "READ" = text fetched and read in this session (HTML or PDF text extracted); "SNIPPET" = search-result summary only; UNVERIFIED = not read. Quotes are short and verbatim from extracted text.

## 1. Chicago Fed NFCI and ANFCI

### Construction (READ: NFCI FAQ PDF, Chicago Fed Letter 386, IJCB 2012 paper + appendix, About/Current-data pages)
- Sources: FAQ https://www.chicagofed.org/-/media/publications/nfci/nfci-faqs-pdf.pdf ; About https://www.chicagofed.org/research/data/nfci/about ; data page https://www.chicagofed.org/research/data/nfci/current-data (and `current-data-aws`); Chicago Fed Letter 386 (Brave and Kelley 2017) https://www.chicagofed.org/-/media/publications/chicago-fed-letter/2017/cfl386-pdf.pdf ; Brave and Butters (2012b) IJCB 8(2):191-239 https://www.ijcb.org/sites/default/files/journal/v8n2/ijcb-v8n2-diagnosing-financial-system-financial-conditions-and-financial-stress.pdf
- Number of variables: 105 now ("The NFCI is a weighted average of 105 indicators of risk, credit, and leverage in the financial system - each expressed relative to its sample average and scaled by its sample standard deviation"). FAQ: "Both indexes contain 46 weekly, 33 monthly, and 26 quarterly indicators." The 2012 IJCB paper used 100 indicators (starting 1973); sample now starts first week of 1971.
- Coverage (FAQ table): business credit 8, shadow banking 9, interbank/securitized debt 10, corporate and government bonds 10, equity and other asset markets 10, banking system 14, commercial paper and repo 14, consumer credit 15, derivatives 15.
- Categories: risk ("volatility and funding risk in the financial sector"), credit ("willingness to both borrow and lend at prevailing prices"), leverage ("debt relative to equity"). FAQ: "Risk measures tend to receive positive weights, while credit and leverage measures tend to receive negative weights." Risk is slightly more than half of absolute contributions; credit about a quarter (NFCI); leverage slightly less than credit.
- Method: mixed-frequency dynamic factor model, state-space, one latent factor, estimated by EM with a missing-value Kalman filter (Doz-Giannone-Reichlin 2006 quasi-ML; also Stock-Watson 2002, Aruoba-Diebold-Scotti 2009). IJCB appendix: "yt is a vector of stationary variables that have been demeaned and standardized"; scale fixed by "restrict the variance of the state disturbances to be 1". FAQ: weights "reflect systemic and dynamic importance" (contemporaneous correlation with other indicators plus ability to explain the factor's dynamics). Chicago Fed Insights (revisions post, https://www.chicagofed.org/publications/blogs/chicago-fed-insights/2020/nfci-revisions, READ via fetch summary): "mixed-frequency dynamic factor model", Kalman filter "concentrates out missing data".
- Standardization/units: NFCI and ANFCI "constructed to average zero with a standard deviation of one" over a sample from 1971 (About page); subindexes also zero mean, unit SD (FAQ; the About page says the subindexes use a 1973 start, the FAQ says 1971 - inconsistent between pages). Positive = tighter than average. Units are standard deviations, NOT interest-rate or GDP units. FRED: "Index, Not Seasonally Adjusted", "Weekly, Ending Friday" (https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NFCI, READ).
- The indicators are transformed individually (levels, level relative to moving average, first differences, 13-week log differences) per the indicator PDF; many are spreads in bp (Haver Analytics source).
- Frequency/publication: weekly; "8:30 a.m. ET on Wednesday, and cover the time period through the previous Friday" (moved to Thursday if holiday). FRED page showed update on Wed 21 Oct 2026 and next release 28 Oct 2026.

### Weights/loadings and how published (READ: indicator PDF https://www.chicagofed.org/-/media/publications/nfci/nfci-indicators-list-pdf.pdf, pdf dated 28 Apr 2026 in its metadata)
- The FAQ and About page text differ on whether weights are published: the About/current-data pages say only "the weights are calculated to capture the relative importance of each indicator" with no numbers, but the Indicators List PDF DOES publish a weight for every one of the 105 indicators for both NFCI and ANFCI. Columns: indicator, start date, transformation, frequency (W/M/Q), NFCI weight, ANFCI weight. Footnote: "The weights have been scaled to sum to 100 in absolute value for ease of presentation." So the published weights are relative loadings (percent of absolute weight), not the raw factor loadings on standardized data; they are a snapshot (the current vintage), with no archive of earlier weights on the pages I read (the FAQ's top-ten lists differ from the current PDF, e.g. FAQ NFCI top weight 3.47 for CMBS spread, current PDF top weight 4.38 for credit-card ABS spread, so weights have changed between versions).
- Grouping: PDF sections are Risk, Credit, Leverage; the nonfinancial-leverage subindex indicators are footnoted. The PDF does not carry a funds-rate or other "market group" label; mapping to rates/equities/dollar/other must be done by us.
- Examples of NFCI weights (percent of absolute weight, current PDF): Risk: Credit-card ABS/5y Tsy spread 4.38; 3-5y AAA CMBS OAS 4.37; Bloomberg Govt Securities Liquidity 3.89; 2y swap/Tsy 3.59; VIX 3.43; 3m swaption vol 2.87; 10y swap spread 2.43; AFE trade-weighted dollar 0.12 (DLN); Credit: Auto ABS spread 4.18; IG CDX 3.93; HY/Baa spread 3.47; Baa/10y Tsy 0.33; Leverage: S&P Financials/S&P 500 relative to 2y MA -3.59; S&P total return (DLN) -0.10; Wilshire 5000 (DLN) -0.06; Fed funds rate (DLV) -0.08; 2y CMT (DLV) -0.15; 10y CMT (DLV) -0.04.
- Implication for groups: rate levels enter only as first differences with tiny weights; the policy rate is nearly absent (weight -0.08); the dollar is 0.12; equities enter via the financials-to-S&P ratio (-3.59), S&P total return (-0.10) and Wilshire (-0.06), VIX 3.43 plus other vol measures. Spreads and survey/credit-standard indicators dominate. A "rates incl. corporate rates" group in NFCI is mostly spreads (swap-Treasury, ABS, CMBS, HY, Baa, mortgage spreads), not yield levels.
- Contributions: Chicago Fed provides weekly XLSX "Contributions" of each indicator and category to NFCI and ANFCI ("The sum of the contributions will result in the current value of the index", current-data page), plus Risk/Credit/Leverage subindex series and, for ANFCI, a "macroeconomic adjustments" component. Download Center (current-data-aws page, READ): NFCI Indexes (CSV), Contributions (XLSX), Indicators List (PDF), Technical Report ("changes to the NFCI as of April 2021", PDF; title seen, NOT read), FAQs, Background Slides. I did not see a downloadable archive of vintages on the Chicago Fed pages.
- Technical report April 2021: UNVERIFIED (only listed). I could not retrieve the URL.

### Revisions, real-time vs revised
- FAQ: "The history of the indexes can change from week to week depending on incoming data and data revisions. Because they include a number of monthly and quarterly data series that are regularly revised, revisions will tend to be more pronounced near the beginning of each month. The ANFCI is additionally influenced by revisions to the adjustment series; and as a result, it will tend to show larger revisions."
- Insights post (revisions): index is re-estimated weekly; revisions come from incoming data, revisions to data, and changes in estimated model parameters; "sizable revisions to the NFCI are most often driven by incoming data"; revision analysis built from archived NFCI releases in ALFRED (St. Louis Fed). So real-time vintages exist in ALFRED (series NFCI, ANFCI); I did not open ALFRED itself (UNVERIFIED how far back).
- Current-data page: chart of mean absolute week-to-week revisions; note that ANFCI history was modestly revised after a commodity index was replaced (2021-ish; date not stated).
- Mixed-frequency handling: for series not yet available, the model estimates likely values from available data and index dynamics (Insights post). So the latest NFCI observation embeds model-based nowcasts of monthly/quarterly components.

### How ANFCI is adjusted (READ: FAQ, CFL 386)
- FAQ: "The ANFCI removes the variation in these indicators attributable to economic activity (as measured by the three-month moving average of the Chicago Fed National Activity Index (CFNAI) and the difference between the unemployment rate and the Congressional Budget Office estimate of the natural rate of unemployment) and inflation (as measured by the 3-month changes in the Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) Price Index and the KR-CRB Spot Commodity Price Index)."
- CFL 386 (2017): "instead of the previous two-step procedure, we use a new simultaneous estimation procedure"; extends history from 1973 to 1971. Decomposition of ANFCI minus NFCI: roughly 30% activity adjustment, 30% inflation adjustment, ~40% re-weighting of indicators. ANFCI puts less absolute weight on credit and a bit more on risk and leverage. Interpretation: positive ANFCI = tighter than macro conditions would suggest.
- Because ANFCI nets out the cyclical component, it is not a "level of financial conditions" in the same sense as NFCI; HHMSW (2010) did the analogous purge on GDP growth and inflation.

## 2. Other candidate indexes

### St. Louis Fed Financial Stress Index (STLFSI4)
- FRED series STLFSI4 (READ, https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/STLFSI4): built from 18 weekly series (seven interest rates, six yield spreads, five other); weekly, ending Friday; index begins late 1993; designed to average zero; STLFSI4 replaced a backward-looking 90-day average SOFR with a forward-looking 90-day SOFR in two spreads. Latest value -0.6328 for week ending 2026-10-16.
- Method (READ: original 2010 appendix https://files.stlouisfed.org/files/htdocs/publications/net/NETJan2010Appendix.pdf): first principal component; series de-meaned, divided by sample SD, coefficients scaled "so that the SD of the index is 1". Coefficients are published in the appendix table (for the 1993-2009 sample): e.g. effective federal funds rate -0.083, 2y Treasury -0.093, 10y Treasury -0.092, (others: Baa 0.035; HY 0.108; ABS BBB 0.105; Baa-10y spread 0.125; LIBOR-OIS 0.122; VIX 0.110; breakeven -0.125). 44.3% of variation explained. Important: the policy rate and Treasury yields enter in LEVELS with NEGATIVE coefficients (low rates = more stress), so a stress index loads on the policy rate with the opposite sign to an FCI. "When the sample changes ... the values of the FSI in the original sample can be changed" (coefficients and standardization re-estimated), so revisions occur. The list/coefficients for the current STLFSI4 (18 series after several revisions) are UNVERIFIED; the FRED blog post on version 4 could not be retrieved (redirect to empty blog).
- Replicable: yes in principle (series list public, method PCA), but the current coefficient table was not seen.

### Kansas City Fed Financial Stress Index (KCFSI)
- FRED KCFSI (READ page): monthly, "Index, Not Seasonally Adjusted"; latest Sept 2026 = -0.98582, updated 6 Oct 2026, next 5 Nov 2026. 14 Sep 2021 release replaced the SNL US Bank index with the S&P US BMI Banks Index, with predicted values (regression on the old index) for 1989-2004.
- Construction (READ via HHMSW 2010 description; SNIPPET for current KC releases): Hakkio and Keeton (2009), 11 standardized monthly variables, principal components (yield spreads and asset-price behavior), history from 1990. KC release PDFs (search snippets only): index has "a mean value of zero and a standard deviation of one"; revisions arise because index is recalculated with new data. Original paper: Hakkio and Keeton (2009), KC Fed Economic Review Q2, pp. 5-50 (URL attempted returned 404; NOT read). Monthly only, so it is too coarse for weekly NFCI comparison.
- Weights public? Not seen; the KC page was not retrievable. UNVERIFIED.

### OFR Financial Stress Index (OFR FSI)
- READ: OFR page https://www.financialresearch.gov/financial-stress-index/ and Monin (2017), OFR WP 17-04 (PDF text) https://www.financialresearch.gov/working-papers/files/OFRwp-17-04_The-OFR-Financial-Stress-Index.pdf
- 33 daily market variables; five categories (credit, equity valuation, funding, safe assets, volatility); contributions by region (US, other advanced, emerging). Dynamic factor model with single latent factor ("essentially corresponds to the first principal component"), indicators standardized (z-scores, signed), weights estimated each date; "the OFR FSI's past values are not re-estimated each time the model is estimated" (no revisions from re-estimation, an advantage for real-time work). Units: unitless, zero = average stress; "positive if the (weighted) average ... is positive". Daily, published with a lag (page: current as of two business days earlier). Data downloadable from the page; weights: paper reports weights (loading vector) for the 33 indicators on 31 Aug 2017 (Table 3B); the website lists indicators; whether current daily weights are downloadable is not stated on the page I read. A revision-history file (27 Jun 2023) exists. Published as Monin (2019), Risks 7(1): 25 (SNIPPET).
- Weights replicable: construction is documented and daily data are public, but some component series may be licensed (UNVERIFIED).

### Bloomberg Financial Conditions Index
- Original description (READ in HHMSW 2010): "equally weighted sum of three major sub-indexes: money market (one-third), bond (one-third), equity (one-third)... Each indicator is standardized ... number of standard deviations above or below the index's 1991 to mid-2007 average", 10 variables, history from 1991, updated daily.
- Arnaut and Bauer (FRBSF 2024, READ): Bloomberg FCI "averages eight indicators from money, bond, and equity markets" (so the variable count changed from 10 to 8); multiplied by -1 to align sign. FEDS Note 2023 (READ): says NFCI and Bloomberg FCI use principal components or dynamic factor analysis (this is a characterization by the authors; HHMSW describes Bloomberg as equal-weighted, so the two descriptions differ - flag).
- Current official methodology, ticker BFCIUS and weights: not found; third-party TradingView/blog items only (SNIPPET) -- UNVERIFIED. Data is on the Bloomberg terminal (licensed). Not publicly replicable as far as I could find.

## 3. Hatzius, Hooper, Mishkin, Schoenholtz, Watson (2010)
- Source READ in full text: NBER WP 16150 PDF https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w16150/w16150.pdf (also US Monetary Policy Forum 2010 report). Note: the paper builds a NEW index; it is not the Goldman Sachs FCI.
- Their new FCI: 45 financial variables (Table 4.1), unbalanced panel PCA; each variable transformed, standardized to mean zero unit variance, regressed on current and two lags of real GDP growth and inflation (GDP deflator), residuals' principal components estimated by least squares over non-missing observations; one-factor FCI weights proportional to the estimated lambdas; 1, 2 and 3 factor versions considered; they deliberately exclude the funds rate ("We chose not to include the fed funds rate or a close substitute") and also test purging the funds rate. Evaluation: predictive regressions of GDP-type growth at 2 and 4 quarters. So the "growth-prediction criterion" applies to evaluation, and the weights are PCA loadings (not regression weights).
- Description of the GS FCI in Section 2.3 (READ): "a weighted sum of a short-term bond yield, a long-term corporate yield, the exchange rate, and a stock market variable (Dudley and Hatzius, 2000; Dudley, Hatzius and McKelvey, 2005). The Federal Reserve Board's macroeconomic model (the FRB/US model), together with Goldman Sachs modeling, were used to determine the weights." Since 2005 long-term corporate yield = 10-year swap + 10-year CDX spread; earlier Moody's A-rated; index = 100 on 20 Oct 2003; "Unlike the other indexes, the Goldman Sachs index exhibits a noticeable downward trend because it uses levels of the financial variables".
- HHMSW states no funds-rate equivalent for any index. For comparison it notes the OECD FCI was normalized so one unit equals the GDP effect of a 1 pp rise in the real long-term rate (OECD FCI: 1-unit increase lowers GDP about 0.6 pp over 4-6 quarters) and the Deutsche Bank index in "percentage point drag on GDP". Bank of Canada MCI: exchange rate weight about one-third of refinancing rate (for Canada).
- Later public GS description (READ): Hatzius (2019), "The Case for a Financial Conditions Index," 22 Feb 2019 (NY Fed conference slides) https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/conference/2019/quantitative_tools/Post_Hatzius_FCIPresentation : weights "Federal Funds Rate: 4.4%, 10-Year Treasury Rate: 45.1%, BBB Credit Spread: 39.6%, Equity Prices: 4.9%, Trade-Weighted Dollar: 6.0%". Also: a chart "Response of US FCI Components to a 5yr UST Fed Shock" ("estimated daily response of FCI and its components (scaled to FCI contributions) to a +1bp shock in 5yr UST, measured as the change in yield in one-hour windows around FOMC announcements") and slides on the link between the funds rate and the FCI loosening over time. The slides, as extracted, give no derivation of the weights and no numeric "bp of funds rate per FCI point"; bar values are not readable in text.
- Rules of thumb: "three 25bp hikes" (Goldman 2015) and "145 bp of index per 100bp of funds rate" are only SNIPPET/secondhand (Business Standard, fetch blocked with 403); the original GS pages (goldmansachs.com "case-for-financial-conditions-index") returned 404/403. UNVERIFIED.
- Arnaut and Bauer (FRBSF 2024, READ): "a policy surprise of 0.1 percentage point leads to an increase in the FCI by about 0.07 index points" on the announcement day (GS FCI), with further increases over two weeks. This implies roughly 0.7 GS-FCI index points per 1 pp of surprise on day 0, an empirical rule of thumb tied to the 30-minute surprise (check the unit definition of their surprise before using). The article gives no funds-rate equivalent; it multiplied Bloomberg by -1 and subtracted 100 from GS for plotting.

## 4. Existing conversions to funds-rate equivalents and critiques

### Conversions / common units (READ unless noted)
- Beaton, Lalonde, Luu (2009), BoC DP 2009-11 https://oar-rao.bank-banque-canada.ca/record/1236/files/dp09-11.pdf (READ, section 6): "Financial Conditions Index in Terms of an Interest Rate Equivalent". Weights = "the estimated impact of a 1-unit change in the relevant financial variable on the level of GDP, averaged over 4-8 quarters, which is then normalized relative to the effect of a unit change in the short-term interest rate on GDP." "A unit increase in the index should be interpreted as the impact that a 100 basis point increase in the short-term policy rate would have on GDP." Normalized around historical mean. Result: MFCI (MUSE model) about 300 bp tightening since 2007Q2; SVECM-based about 350 bp; up to 625 bp if SVECM shocks are valued against MUSE's funds-rate multiplier. ZLB may amplify by up to 40% (abstract). This is the output-equivalent lens in the plan; it also shows the large sensitivity to which model supplies the funds-rate multiplier.
- Guichard and Turner (2008) (OECD FCI; interest-rate-equivalent in terms of a long-term rate, as described by Beaton et al.; not read directly).
- SF Fed Proxy Funds Rate (READ page https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/data-and-indicators/proxy-funds-rate/): follows Doh and Choi (2016), Choi-Doh-Foerster-Martinez (2022), Foerster-Martinez (2024); 12 financial variables, principal components, first three components mapped to funds-rate levels using pre-2008 correlations; until Dec 2008 "the mapping is nearly exact by construction", afterward it diverges; weekly and monthly; CSV and Excel downloads; updated 20 Oct 2026. Already in funds-rate units: an anchor needing no conversion. Full 12-variable list not given on the page (UNVERIFIED).
- Sack and Swanson (Brookings, 18 Oct 2023) https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-impact-of-federal-reserve-policy-on-the-feds-financial-conditions-index/ (READ): 2-variable VAR of the Fed's FCI and the 2-year Treasury yield, high-frequency identification (Bauer-Swanson 2023; data Swanson-Jayawickrema 2023). Rule of thumb: "the Fed's FCI initially tightens by 20 basis points for a 25-basis-point upward shift" in the 2-year yield, peaking at 33 bp about a quarter later; policy alone would have raised the FCI by about 3.75 pp at its peak versus an actual 2.5 pp rise (March 2022 to July 2023, funds rate +5.25 pp). The page as extracted does not name FCI-G; treat "Fed's FCI" as FCI-G only after checking. Closest existing funds-rate-style translation for FCI-G (a response ratio, ie. transmission-equivalent lens).
- Atlanta Fed macroblog (18 Aug 2026) https://www.atlantafed.org/research-and-data/publications/policy-hub-macroblog/2026/08/18/monetary-policy-stance-and-financial-conditions (READ): uses FCI-G (excluding policy-rate component) and the GS FCI alongside a Monetary Policy Index (2/3 standardized EFFR + 1/3 standardized balance-sheet liquidity); explicitly NOT converted to policy-rate terms. Shows the combination of FCI-G, GS and policy variables is current in staff work.
- FEDS Note (Ajello et al. 2023, READ): FCI-G is in GDP-growth units ("a reading of 1 means a drag of about 100 basis points on GDP growth over the next year"); weights are FRB/US dynamic multipliers (difference of four-quarter-ahead and current multipliers); seven variables (fed funds rate, 10y Treasury, 30y mortgage, BBB corporate yield, Dow Jones total stock market, Zillow house prices, nominal broad dollar). Authors state NFCI and Bloomberg use "principal components or dynamic factor analysis"; statistical weights "do not necessarily have a straightforward economic interpretation"; GS weighs by "contribution to expected economic activity" but "typically do not take into account the lagged effects"; statistical FCIs "tend to tighten sharply" in brief stress episodes. No funds-rate equivalent in the note. https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/a-new-index-to-measure-us-financial-conditions-20230630.html
- Morgan Stanley five-variable index (Tobias 2026): not found in search; UNVERIFIED. Wu-Xia shadow rate: not read this session (only cited in plan).

### Critiques of model- and PCA-based weights
- Koop and Korobilis (2014), "A new index of financial conditions," European Economic Review 71: 101-116. SNIPPET (abstract only): FAVAR-type approach with time-varying parameters and stochastic volatility and dynamic model averaging or selection so weights and included variables change over time. Full text NOT read.
- Arregui, Elekdag, Gelos, Lafarguette, Seneviratne (2018), IMF WP/18/15 (author list incl. Gelos per search; IMF page lists five authors), "Can Countries Manage Their Financial Conditions Amid Globalization?" SNIPPET only (abstract: global factor explains about 20-40 percent of domestic FCI variation; countries retain control mainly through monetary policy). Their FCI construction and any critique of PCA weights NOT read (IMF PDF fetch blocked/403).
- Brave and Butters (2012b) (READ): compare NFCI with alternatives built from fewer variables or different sample; factor loadings change materially across subsets (sign changes for yield-curve variables in sub-sample/"nine-variable" index). Text lines 1273-1372 only skimmed.
- Beaton et al. (READ): notes growth-based models vs. PCA; also shows ranges across two models (300 vs 625 bp).
- HHMSW (READ): footnote 19 reports the non-purged FCI had two local minima in the least-squares problem, i.e., unbalanced-panel PCA weights can be non-unique; purged vs. unpurged versions differ materially in 2009 (conditional on activity and inflation, financial conditions still a drag).
- Monin (2017) (READ) distinguishes FSIs from FCIs: FCIs focus on link to real economy; FSIs on distress; FSIs mostly market prices, FCIs also flows and stocks. Also contrasts index re-estimation: OFR no revisions; other FSIs re-estimate full history.

## 5. Implications (10 lines)
1. NFCI/ANFCI are in SD units (zero mean, unit SD since 1971), with no model-based mapping to GDP or funds rate; a funds-rate equivalent must come from outside the index: either a regression slope of weekly NFCI changes on policy news/shocks (transmission-equivalent) or a mapping from each indicator's contribution to a funds-rate response.
2. Chicago publishes weekly, per-indicator contributions (XLSX) and a snapshot of 105 weights per index (percent of absolute weight), so a by-group (rates/equities/dollar/other) decomposition of NFCI in SD units is feasible directly; map volatility to "other", and note rate LEVELS carry almost no weight (funds rate -0.08, 10y CMT -0.04, dollar 0.12).
3. Because NFCI is dominated by spreads, surveys, leverage and volatility, most indicators barely respond to a funds-rate surprise in a 30-minute window; a funds-rate equivalent will be low-precision and event windows can only cover weekly aggregates, so use weekly (Friday-to-Friday) regressions and report bands.
4. Weights and history are revised weekly (parameters change slightly; ANFCI revises more; monthly/quarterly data revisions near month start); ALFRED stores vintages, so use real-time vintages for any real-time claim and state the vintage date for the levels comparison.
5. Latest NFCI weeks contain Kalman-filter nowcasts of monthly and quarterly series; treat the last 4-6 weeks as provisional when reporting "current readings".
6. ANFCI removes activity/inflation-related variation (CFNAI, unemployment gap, PCE, KR-CRB); it answers a different question (conditional on macro), so include it but label it as such and expect different levels (and bigger revisions).
7. Include in scope: FCI-G, GS (replicated, weights 4.4/45.1/39.6/4.9/6.0 from Hatzius 2019, derivation not public), NFCI/ANFCI, SF Fed proxy funds rate (already in funds-rate units; anchor). Stress contrast: OFR FSI (daily, no re-estimation, documented method, weights in paper) is the cleanest; STLFSI4 (weekly) is the easiest to align with NFCI but loads the policy rate and Treasury yields with negative sign (stress rises when rates are low), which makes a funds-rate equivalent ill-defined.
8. Exclude Bloomberg FCI (weights undisclosed, count changed 10 to 8, descriptions differ) and KCFSI (monthly, KC page not read); Morgan Stanley index not located (UNVERIFIED).
9. Beaton-Lalonde-Luu is the template for an output-equivalent funds-rate unit (normalize by the GDP effect of the funds rate in a model; sensitivity 300 vs 625 bp across models); Sack-Swanson gives a transmission ratio for the Fed FCI (20 bp per 25 bp of 2-year yield, peak 33 bp) and Arnaut-Bauer gives about 0.07 GS points per 0.1 pp surprise: both are good cross-checks for the conversions.
10. Still to do: read Brave-Butters 2011 full text, the Chicago technical report (April 2021), ALFRED coverage, Koop-Korobilis and Arregui et al. full texts, current STLFSI4 coefficients, and obtain the original GS notes (Dudley-Hatzius 2000; 2015 "three hikes" note) before quoting any GS rule of thumb.
