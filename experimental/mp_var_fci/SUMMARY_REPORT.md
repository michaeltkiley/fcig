# Does a market-based financial conditions index make monetary-policy impulse responses more robust? Summary report

31 Oct 2026. Exploratory; every number is reproducible from the scripts listed at the end; decisions MP-A1 to MP-A11 and the full working log are in `MP_QA.md`. Citations were read in full text (Gertler-Karadi 2015, Lunsford 2015, Colombo 2025, Bauer-Swanson 2023,
Nakamura-Steinsson 2018, Miranda-Agrippino-Ricco 2021, Caldara-Herbst 2019); where I could not read something (Gertler-Karadi's replication code) the text says so.

## 1. Bottom line
1. **The market index, as built, does not make the responses more robust, larger, or less sample-dependent.** Adding it to a monetary VAR changes the unemployment response little (Cholesky: mean absolute change 0.04 to 0.15 pp) and does not reconcile the long and short samples.
2. **What does work is a credit-type variable**, as Caldara-Herbst predict: adding the excess bond premium (EBP) or the Chicago Fed NFCI removes the significantly wrong-signed unemployment response after 1994 and cuts the gap between samples by more than half. The index adds nothing measurable on top of them.
3. **Full-sample identification with FOMC-only surprises is weak** for the 2-year yield and for path surprises (F 2 to 6; sets unbounded), as in the literature. The funds rate with the current-meeting surprise (MP1) is the exception: first stage 8 to 10 (robust), strong on every other F definition.
4. **The Greenbook purge does not help.** Path surprises (NS, ED4) are predictable from Greenbook forecasts (R2 0.16 and 0.21); MP1 is not (0.06). Purging lowers or leaves unchanged every first stage.
5. **A feasible contribution remains**, narrower than the original plan: test the index against the credit spread in the instrument-valid setting (funds rate, MP1, with the full sample and the Caldara-Herbst window as cases), and use event-study evidence for the financial side after 2008.

## 2. The object and the question
The market index (note `fci_note/`): weights on the 10-year yield, equity valuation and the real dollar from their responses to Nakamura-Steinsson policy news in FOMC windows; unit = a policy move raising the 2-year by 1 pp; r*-gap baseline; two-variable VAR with the unemployment gap for dynamics.
The project adds core PCE (raw monthly annualized) and a policy variable and asks, for Cholesky, Nakamura-Steinsson (NS) and Bauer-Swanson (BS) identification: does the index make the monetary-shock responses (a) more robust to the scheme, (b) larger, (c) less dependent on the sample, (d) less dependent on the Fed information effect.

## 3. What was learned, in order
**Cholesky (steps 2, 2d, 2e).** Unemployment gap, core PCE, policy variable, index (index last). Long window (1973 or 1976 to 2026): small conventional response (+0.16 to +0.19 pp at 12 months per +1 pp funds rate). **Every window starting in 1994: wrong sign and large** (-0.4 to -1.1 pp per +1 pp; -0.4 per +1 pp 2-year), already before the zero lower bound (1994-2008m11: -0.85, band -1.24 to -0.26) and after dummying the ZLB months. One-sd funds-rate innovations are 0.12-0.15 pp (vs 0.48 long) and are followed by sustained hikes (+2.7 to +3.4 pp at 12 months). The index changes these responses little.
**Instruments (steps 3 to 4d).** NS and my own public-data purge correlate 0.94. First stage on the VAR residual of the 2-year (four-variable system, 12 lags): NS 3.5 [6.4], ED4 5.6 [7.6], FF4 0.4; funds rate: MP1 9.3 [34], NS 4.7. Proxy-SVAR with the weak instruments: Anderson-Rubin sets unbounded or very wide.
**Literature on first stages.** Gertler-Karadi report F = 21.5 (robust 17.5) for the 1-year rate with FF4 on instruments from 1991, and **found the 2-year weak** (F 0.9 to 5.2), which is why their baseline is the 1-year. Bauer-Swanson: FOMC-only 7.69 raw and 2.44 orthogonalized; 15.83 and 10.23 once Chair speeches and testimony are added and events extend back to 1988. Miranda-Agrippino-Ricco: 15.3 for their Greenbook-purged FF4 on the 1-year rate (1990-2009). Caldara-Herbst: reliability 0.11 for their funds-rate proxy and **0.01 for a 2-year proxy** (informative only with a tight prior). Lunsford: the right F projects the proxy on all VAR errors, critical values 7 to 9; Colombo: the shock-based F is weakly larger than the anchor-based one.
Our numbers sit in the same range as theirs for the same instrument type. The strong literature results come from a specific choice (1-year rate, FF4, window from 1991, additional events), not from a different construction of NS (my rebuild from the Nakamura-Steinsson package correlates 0.97 with the project's factor).

## 5. Is there a feasible path?
Yes, but it is a different project from the one first described: **the market index is not the variable that rescues monetary-policy identification; credit is.** What remains feasible and informative:
1. **Test the index against the credit spread** in the instrument-valid settings (funds rate with MP1, 1994-2026 and the 1994-2007 window; 2-year as the units robustness case). Systems with EBP and with and without the index; report weak-instrument-robust sets and, if wanted, Caldara-Herbst's Bayesian priors on proxy reliability (with the prior sensitivity shown). Early answer: no gain from the index on top of EBP/NFCI.
2. **Policy reaction to the index (Caldara-Herbst's reaction-function result):** do the funds rate and the 2-year react within the month to the equity-dollar-yield index, beyond the spread? Not yet estimated; the proxy-SVAR impact vector gives it directly.
3. **After 2008, event-study evidence only:** the index is built from event-window responses (identification strong), and the macro VAR is descriptive.
4. **Needs new data to go further:** Chair speeches and testimony surprises and the 1988-93 events (Bauer-Swanson), payroll-surprise and Treasury-skewness predictors; with them a full-sample 2-year instrument might reach F of 10 to 16 as in their paper.
**Caveats.** The Anderson-Rubin sets are conditional on the VAR coefficients (too tight); MP1 only approximates Caldara-Herbst's proxy and the note's event set includes 9 unscheduled meetings; the CH window has 162 months; my Greenbook purge covers 205 events (1996-2020); I used a frequentist estimator, not their Bayesian one; no formal test of differences between systems was run; Gertler-Karadi's lag length is unknown.
