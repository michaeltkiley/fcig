## 1. Abstract (7 sentences, no section label other than "Abstract")

Order of moves, observed: context (1), contrast with the past (2), purpose (3), main result (4), restatement of the result in plain words (5), a closing sentence on intuition/implications (6). No robustness sentence. No numbered contribution list.

- Opens with facts, not with a literature gap: "Inflation was low and stable in the United States during the first two decades of the 21 st century and broke out of its stable range in 2021."
- Question stated as "examines the extent to": "This analysis examines the extent to which the experience from 2000-2019 should lead a Bayesian decisionmaker to update their assessment of inflation dynamics."
- Result stated as a conditional: "Given a prior for inflation dynamics consistent with 1960-1999 data, a Bayesian decisionmaker would not update their view of inflation persistence in light of 2000-2019 data unless they placed very low weight on their prior information."
- The result is then restated with a plain-language signpost: "In other words, 21st century data contains very little information to dissuade a Bayesian decisionmaker of the view that inflation fluctuations are persistent, or “unanchored”."
- Last sentence is short and gives no result: "The intuition for, and implications of, this finding are discussed."
- Numbers: only years/periods ("2000-2019", "1960-1999"). No coefficients, no percentages, no magnitudes. Sentence length: 2 short (about 15-20 words) and the rest about 30-40 words.
- Passive voice appears in the closing sentence ("are discussed"); the abstract uses third person ("This analysis examines"), not "I".

## 2. Introduction

Section 1, first paragraph in full: "Consumer price inflation in the United States, as measured by the Consumer Price Index, jumped to just above 7 percent in the twelve months ending in December 2021. Inflation in 2021 reached the highest level seen since the early 1980s. The jump in inflation outside of the range experienced over several decades has raised questions regarding the speed with which, or the degree to which, inflation may return to the 2-percent range consistent with the Federal Reserve’s inflation objective. The answers to these questions hinge on the nature of the inflation process, including its persistence and the impact of economic slack on the inflation outlook."

- Second paragraph frames the issue as a method choice, not a gap: "A critical consideration in any work pursuing these questions is how to weigh data from different time periods."
- Alternatives are presented neutrally and credited: "Substantial bodies of research have considered both approaches."
- Own approach announced with a plain verb: "This research pursues a different tack. The approach herein is the one a Bayesian decisionmaker would follow."
- Contribution paragraph (third paragraph): "The approach of the Bayesian decisionmaker has several advantages relative to other approaches. The first advantage is that the approach has not been used to inform an assessment of inflation dynamics and hence provides a new (and different) perspective. The second advantage is that the approach is a natural way to combine prior information/experience with recent data, in two senses:" ... "Finally, the approach yields insights regarding the information in macroeconomic data that are likely broadly relevant in macroeconomics—and therefore can inform future research on other questions."
  - Observed: contribution is framed as advantages of the approach (ordinal list "first... second... Finally"), not as "we contribute". The first advantage is novelty of the method ("has not been used").
- Results are previewed in the introduction, with a confidence claim: "The results that emerge from the analysis are very clear. Data over the period 2000-2019 provide very little information with which to update prior views on the inflation process, at least with respect to inflation persistence or the “anchoring” of inflation."
- A bounded implication is stated up front: "A direct implication of this result is that the inflation process may signal substantial persistence, suggesting the high inflation of 2021 may continue in 2022."
- Intuition is previewed too: "The intuition for this finding is straightforward."
- Literature is placed after the results preview under an inline run-in label: "Previous literature: The analysis is related to the empirical literature examining the factors that determine the inflation process..." Roadmap also uses a run-in label: "Structure of the remaining sections: Section 2 discusses data and the framework a Bayesian decisionmaker ... Section 3 presents results, intuition, and implications. Section 4 concludes."
- Literature treatment is a long, cite-dense survey (many parenthetical author-year strings, e.g. "(e.g., Williams, 2006a,b; Kiley, 2008b; Ball and Mazumder, 2011, 2019; Stock, 2011; ...)"), with frequent self-citation (Kiley 2000, 2008a,b, 2015a,b, 2020a,b).

## 3. Sentence-level prose habits

- Person: third person and "the analysis"/"the approach"/"the decisionmaker" as grammatical subject; "herein" is frequent. Examples: "The approach herein is the one a Bayesian decisionmaker would follow." "The empirical work herein links anchoring to the persistence in inflation". First person singular ("I") does not appear in the text I read. First person plural appears once: "for this reason, we will refer to the inflation objective in the United States, as measured by the CPI, as in the range of 2 percent."
- Active verbs with the analysis as subject: "The study analyses inflation in the United States." "The analysis focuses on the Consumer Price Index (CPI)".
- Results stated directly, with numbers and "First... Second..." structure: "Two results emerge clearly." / "First, the degree of persistence in the posterior estimates is high in all cases except those with very little weight on the 20th century prior." / "Second, the slope of the Phillips Curve is consistently smaller in absolute value in the posterior estimates of the parameters—irrespective of the weight on the prior."
- Numbers in prose: "In the monthly estimates, the coefficient on lagged inflation exceeds 0.9 with equal weights on prior and data, exceeds 0.85 when the weight on the prior is 0.20, and is near 0.7 when the weight on the prior is 0.05".
- Evaluative/intuition words recur: "The results are clear—but they are also very intuitive." "This is intuitive—the data do not provide examples of what would happen should inflation deviate from its mean". "Intuitively, essentially zero weight on the prior information returns the least-squares estimate."
- Plain-language restatement signals: "In words, inflation did not deviate from its average value much over 2000-2019" and "Heuristically, this strength could be termed the degree of conviction in the prior information."
- Em-dashes are used heavily for asides and glosses ("—i.e., ...", "—although ..."). Frequent "i.e.," and "e.g.," in parentheses.
- Equations introduced by a sentence ending in "as in equation (1)" or a colon, and followed by a "where"-style gloss sentence: "The analysis considers estimates of a Phillips Curve in which inflation (∆𝑝(𝑡)) depends on its own lags and the (lagged) unemployment rate (𝑢(𝑡)) as in equation (1) (in which a constant term is suppressed):" then "b(j) are the coefficients governing persistence, 𝑎 is the slope of the Phillips curve, and e(t) is the residual reflecting “supply” shocks and other unmodeled factors".
- Equations are interpreted after the fact: "Notice in equation (3) that the Bayesian decisionmaker estimates the parameters as the matrix-weighted average of their prior information and the least-squares estimate". "Equation (3) suggests a natural approach to considering different degrees of conviction regarding the prior information."
- Scare quotes on terms of art: "“anchored”", "“unanchored”", "“luck”", "“weight”", "“fit”", "“flatter”". Stated reason for simplicity choices: "this specification is simplest" / "the simpler approach is adopted herein."

## 4. Robustness, sensitivity, alternatives, limitations (every instance found)

Tone observed: reported flatly, as short statements that results are "similar"; no apology; no "we acknowledge". Placement: scattered in the data section and method section as one-clause assurances, plus one short subsection (3.3) and one limitation passage inside the intuition subsection. There is no separate robustness section or appendix; no appendix at all.

Data section (Section 2.1):
- "The results are generally similar for the overall CPI, reflecting the correlation between overall and core CPI (e.g., Kiley, 2008a)."
- "The results are also similar when the price index considered is the chain-weighted price index for personal consumption expenditures (PCE prices)."
- Frequency check labelled as robustness in passing: "as the Phillips Curve analysis will consider data at both the monthly and annual frequency as one robustness check."
- Sample exclusion, with candour about an unresolved issue: "The years 2020 and 2021 are excluded from the table and the estimation sample, reflecting the unprecedented (and unusual) effects of the COVID-19 pandemic; econometric work will almost surely explore various ways to treat these unusual years in emerging research." (footnote 3 cites Lenza and Primiceri (2020) and Schorfheide and Song (2021) on "the potential sensitivity of macroeconometric estimates to the COVID-19 pandemic period".)

Approach section (2.2):
- Alternative prior on the error variance, dismissed on grounds of clarity: "This alternative approach yields essentially identical results for reasonable priors on σ , but yields more complicated algebraic expressions that slightly impede intuition for those less familiar with Bayesian least squares. As a result, the simpler approach is adopted herein."
- Lag structure: "alternative choices for the lag structure yielded similar results (see section 3.3), and this specification is simplest."
- Footnote 4, convention caveat: "This convention makes the slope coefficients somewhat more comparable (albeit still not strictly comparable, reflecting how time-averaging would affect impact estimates at different frequencies)."
- Weights on the prior are the main sensitivity device, built into the design: "Choice of weights 𝒘: To consider decreasing levels of conviction in the relevance of the 20 th century prior (i.e., looser priors), four values for weights on the prior are considered".

Results section:
- Limitation of the method, stated within the intuition subsection: "This comparison also highlights a potential weakness of the approach of a Bayesian decisionmaker for a researcher that views a structural break as likely."
- Followed by a plain explanation and a recommendation of other methods: "This approach is consistent with the notion that the future may look different than embedded in prior information, but not consistent with a structural break—which would imply prior information has no value." and "...but also may wish to consider alternative approaches that allow for structural breaks or time-varying parameters."
- Qualification of the main claim: "Nonetheless, the coefficient on the lags of inflation is less than one—so the process is not of the “accelerationist” type and would generally imply that deviations of inflation from its average may be long-lived but are ultimately transitory."
- Section 3.3 "Robustness and Implications" is the only robustness passage; it opens with a backward reference, then reports: "As noted above, the basic results do not depend upon the specific lag structure assumed for inflation in the results reported in table 2. To illustrate the robustness of the results to alternative specifications, table 3 considers a slightly more flexible lag structure". Result: "The results are substantially similar to those for the simpler specification in table 2, with 𝑏(1) + 𝑏(2) estimated at essentially the same values as those for 𝑏(1) in table 2."
- Forecast implication hedged: "Generally speaking, the results herein suggest a higher degree of persistence is plausible, pointing to potentially higher inflation in 2022."
- Hedged claim in the intro: "at least with respect to inflation persistence or the “anchoring” of inflation" and "the inflation process may signal substantial persistence".
- Not examined (stated as future work, not as limitation): "But much of this work is similar to the approach herein, focusing on inflation persistence with little direct attention to expectations. Future work can consider the implications of the approach herein for expectations per se."

Summary (INTERPRETATION): robustness is treated as reassurance reported in a clause or sentence ("similar", "essentially identical"), the paper gives one real limitation of the method and states it plainly in the body rather than in the conclusion; hedges use "may", "plausible", "potentially"; no apologies. I cannot say from this text whether the IJCB version adds a robustness section.

## 5. Forward and backward references

Common, short, and signposted. Observed:
- Forward: "alternative choices for the lag structure yielded similar results (see section 3.3)"; "Section 2 discusses data ... Section 3 presents results, intuition, and implications. Section 4 concludes."; "Two aspects of the summary statistics will prove important in understanding the results."; "These three sample periods will be referred to as the full sample, the pre-2000 sample, and the post-1999 sample."; "With this background, the approach involves choosing the lag specification in the Phillips Curve, a choice of the prior distribution and consideration of alternative weights on this prior distribution."
- Backward: "Recall from equation (4) that the posterior estimates are the matrix-weighted average of the prior mean and least-squares estimate"; "Recall that this matrix is σ (𝑋 𝑋) and its inverse is σ 𝑋 𝑋."; "As noted above, the basic results do not depend upon the specific lag structure"; "(i.e., the first approach above)"; "This intuition is the same as that above—inflation has been stable and hence the data do not differentiate much between a specification with a large or small coefficient on lagged inflation."
- Count (approximate, from my reading): about 4 forward and about 6 backward references in 14 pages. Section 3.3 and the intuition subsection each open by pointing backward.

## 6. Section openings, closings, conclusion

- Structure: numbered sections 1-4 with decimal subsections (2.1, 2.2, 3.1-3.3). Subsection titles are plain nouns: "Data", "Empirical Approach", "Estimates", "Intuition", "Robustness and Implications".
- Openings are declarative and concrete, with no preamble: Section 2.1 "The study analyses inflation in the United States." Section 3.1 "The results for posterior estimates of the Phillips Curve parameters conditional on data from 2000-2019 by the Bayesian decisionmaker are shown in figure 3 and table 2. Two results emerge clearly." Section 3.2 "The results are clear—but they are also very intuitive."
- Section 3.1 closes with an interpretive sentence, not a transition: "...may be long-lived but are ultimately transitory."
- Section 3.3 (and Section 3) ends on a forecast implication, not a summary: "Generally speaking, the results herein suggest a higher degree of persistence is plausible, pointing to potentially higher inflation in 2022."
- Conclusion (Section 4) is two paragraphs, about 170 words in all. Paragraph 1 repeats the abstract almost word for word: "This analysis examined the extent to which the experience from 2000-2019 should lead a Bayesian decisionmaker to update their assessment of inflation dynamics." (tense shifted from "examines" to "examined"; remaining sentences match the abstract, including "In other words, 21st century data contains very little information to dissuade a Bayesian decisionmaker of the view that inflation fluctuations are persistent, or “unanchored”.") Paragraph 2 broadens: "The idea that data over short sample periods may provide limited information regarding macroeconomic relationships may be relevant for other areas in macroeconomics." and ends "These findings suggest macroeconomists may find useful a Bayesian approach that examines the information in the data more thoroughly than is common in empirical macroeconomics."
- No summary of robustness, no limitations paragraph, no policy recommendation, no "future work" list in the conclusion.
- Acknowledgements/thanks: none seen in the extracted text (the asterisked footnote on the title page gives affiliation and a disclaimer only: "The views expressed herein are those of the author, and do not reflect those of the Federal Reserve Board or its staff."). Could not determine whether the IJCB version adds acknowledgements.

## 7. Tables, figures, notation, footnotes

- Tables and figures are placed at the end (pages 19-25), after the references, in working-paper style. In the text they are cited by lower-case lead-in with a one-sentence description: "Figure 1 presents the data on inflation and the unemployment rate." "Table 1 presents some summary statistics on inflation and the unemployment rate." "Figure 2 presents the prior distributions for the coefficient on the lags of inflation and the slope of the Phillips Curve for these alternative weights."
- Text reads the figure for the reader: "Inflation rose over the late 1960s and was both higher and more volatile over the 1970s."; "Figure 4 presents a dynamic simulation of the estimates from 2000-2019 in the top panel and the residuals implied by each estimate in the bottom panel".
- Table notes: every table and figure has a "Source: Bureau of Labor Statistics and author’s calculations." line; tables add "Note:" defining sample periods. Table titles are descriptive and plain ("Table 2: Estimation of posterior of parameters"). The estimating equation is repeated above Tables 2 and 3.
- Notation: lower-case for scalars with a time argument in parentheses rather than subscripts ("∆𝑝(𝑡)", "𝑢(𝑡 − 1)", "b(j)"); Γ, V, X, Y, E for matrices with sizes given in parentheses ("Y (Tx1, where T is the number of observations)"). Equations numbered (1)-(4), right-aligned. Four equations in total in the text.
- Footnotes: 4 in the text, used for side-literature and caveats (fn 1 "Theoretical modeling has also considered possible factors, e.g., Kiley (2007)."; fn 2 on city- and state-level Phillips curve studies; fn 3 on COVID sensitivity; fn 4 on the annual-rate convention).
- Defined labels in bold-less run-in form with a bullet and short label plus colon: "Lag specification in the Phillips Curve:", "Choice for prior", "Choice of weights 𝒘:" (bulleted list of three design choices).

## 8. Notable absences (observed, within this working paper)

- No "I"/"my"; no "we contribute"/"our contribution" phrasing; no "this paper makes three contributions" list.
- No numerical results in the abstract.
- No separate robustness section or appendix; no appendix at all; no limitations section.
- No hedging boilerplate such as "caveat", "we caution"; the one explicit limitation is called "a potential weakness".
- No acknowledgements, no data/code availability statement in the extracted text (could not rule out that they exist in the IJCB version).
- No policy-recommendation section; no formal definitions/propositions; no running examples.
