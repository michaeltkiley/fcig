# Literature: high-frequency monetary shocks and mixed-frequency models (search of 1 Nov 2026)

Question (user): window-identified shock propagated through a daily-financial / monthly-macro MIDAS-VAR is "such an obvious application that either our approach is wrong or it has been done."
Answer in short: each ingredient exists; no paper found that combines (1) a window impact vector with macro entries zero, (2) daily propagation of financial conditions, and (3) monthly macro equations driven by monthly averages of daily financial conditions. The closest papers pass the shock to macro by a DIRECT regression (Francis-Ghysels-Owyang; Chudik-Georgiadis; Chudik-Kilian) or by aggregating daily shocks into a monthly proxy (Gazzani-Vicondoa; Kilian). Chudik and Kilian (Feb 2026) explicitly leave sparse announcement shocks (FOMC) to future research.

## Second reading (1 Nov 2026)
- **Francis-Ghysels-Owyang (2011)** [read in full]. Shock: the FOMC-day innovation of a daily AR model of the effective funds rate, 1960-2009 (robustness: Romer-Romer and a monthly-VAR shock, timed to the FOMC day). No window or futures identification. One equation per macro variable and horizon (local-projection style): Y(t+h) on the FOMC-day shock, MIDAS-weighted pre-meeting days, MIDAS-weighted past daily shocks and lagged Y. No financial-conditions channel, no daily dynamics. Results: equal-weight OLS gives puzzles (tightening raises IP and employment, lowers unemployment); with MIDAS timing, real effects are insignificant, the core-price puzzle disappears, core inflation falls significantly only after about 21 months, inflation expectations rise. A direct-regression ancestor of our Chudik-Georgiadis check, with the same weak result.
- **Chudik-Kilian (2026)** [read sections 1-3 and 6]. Only the shock and the aggregated outcome are needed (no full model); unknown aggregation weights allowed; pooled estimators (one coefficient per monthly lag for a given within-month shock pattern) beat mean-group ones, and pooled ARDL/VARDL (adding lags of the outcome) beat pooled DL in their Monte Carlo. Causal reading requires the shock to be uncorrelated with other innovations. Conclusion: **sparse announcement shocks (FOMC, OPEC) are explicitly left to future research**, citing Kilian (2024) and **Lee and Sekhposyan (2024), Texas A&M working paper** (text still not found).
- **Kilian (2024, JEDC; WP 2023)** [read sections 1, 3, 5]. Under a random walk, a surprise on day d of a T-day month raises that month's average by (T-d+1)/T and leaves a second measured surprise next month; summing surprises within the month (standard practice) gives a different proxy (correlation 0.42 for OPEC) and can make a weak instrument look strong. Recommends: estimate on the period where surprises exist; Anderson-Rubin inference; check what the identified "news" shock really is (OPEC surprises are mostly demand news). He notes Caldara-Herbst find end-of-month prices too noisy, and Gertler-Karadi's partial timing correction; suggests applying the method to monetary policy. Our MODEL with a flat daily path reproduces his accounting.
- **Bauer-Swanson (2023)** [read sections 5.1-5.4; Figs. 2, 3, 8 viewed]. Benchmark (Fig. 8; VAR 1973-2020, instrument 1988-2019, FOMC + Chair speeches, orthogonalized; 25 bp on the 2-year): unemployment +0.05 pp at about 10 months, 90% band excludes zero from about month 3 to about 18-20; CPI -0.05% on impact, about -0.12% at 24 m, -0.2% over five years, band excludes zero throughout but narrowly in months 5-12 ("bootstrapped 90% standard-error bands", not weak-IV robust). Fig. 3: orthogonalizing raises responses about fourfold (IP trough -1.4% vs -0.35% per 25 bp) but the 2-year turns negative after about 7 months.
- **Caldara-Herbst (2016 FEDS / 2019 AEJ: Macro)** [read sections 4, 6]. 18 bp funds-rate shock lowers IP about 0.75% after 2.5 years in the trivariate model with the excess bond premium; without spreads, about no IP response; no price response (prices dropped for that reason); policy reacts to spreads within the month.
- **Identification lessons for us** [inference]: (1) our exclusion restriction is the identifying assumption and must be stated; (2) temporal aggregation is handled by construction; (3) the financial-to-macro link is estimated from all variation in financial conditions; (4) the base case should use the sample where the shock exists (1994+); (5) the permutation null shows HAC bands for sparse-shock direct regressions are far too narrow.
