Goldman Sachs–style Financial Conditions Index · daily

A public-data Goldman Sachs FCI

Goldman's U.S. Financial Conditions Index is a levels index around 100: higher means tighter financial conditions, lower means easier. It is built from the federal funds rate, the 10‑year Treasury yield, the BBB corporate spread, equity valuations and the dollar, with weights Goldman has published. This page rebuilds it every weekday from public data. Differences between two dates measure the tightening or easing in between. See also the Fed's FCI‑G replication.

How the index is built

Goldman's weights (Hatzius, New York Fed presentation, Feb 2019, repeated in Goldman research in 2021) reflect each variable's estimated effect on activity, not its volatility. Rates and spreads enter in percentage points; equity valuations and the dollar in log points (100 × ln).

FCI = 100 + Σ w·(X − mean X) = 100 + 0.044·ΔFFR + 0.451·ΔY10 + 0.396·ΔBBB − 0.049·100Δln(CAPE) + 0.060·100Δln(USD)

The reference means are taken over 2000–2025 and then frozen, so history does not revise as data arrive. Differences between two dates do not depend on the normalization.

"One point" is the move in each variable that alone tightens the index by one point. Weights are those published in 2019; I have not verified whether Goldman has changed them since.

Sources and differences from Goldman

  • Rates: FRED effective fed funds (DFF) and 10‑year Treasury (DGS10).
  • BBB spread: ICE BofA BBB option‑adjusted spread (FRED BAMLC0A4CBBB). FRED shows only the last three years, so the page stores the history it has collected; before Oct 2023 it uses Moody's Baa yield less the 10‑year, level‑matched at the splice. Goldman's exact spread definition is not public.
  • Equity valuation: Shiller's CAPE from multpl.com, made daily with the S&P 500 as described above. Shiller's own spreadsheet is no longer updated promptly, so it is not used.
  • Dollar: the Federal Reserve's nominal broad dollar index (FRED DTWEXBGS, with DTWEXB and TWEXBMTH before 2006 and 1995). Goldman uses its own trade‑weighted index, a main reason a level match is only approximate.
  • Level: Goldman's exact normalization is proprietary (an October 2003 base date has been reported for the historical series, and an average of 100 since 2000 more recently). The replication uses a 2000–2025 mean of 100.

Published values quoted above come from public material (Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco, March 2024; Goldman Sachs Global Views, Dec 2023; New York Fed Investor Advisory Committee, July 2022; NYC Comptroller and SanCap presentations). Goldman has not provided a downloadable series, so no point‑by‑point comparison of daily levels is possible here. This is not a Goldman Sachs product.
