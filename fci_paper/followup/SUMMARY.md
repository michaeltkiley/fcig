# Follow-up analyses after the advisor review (11 Nov 2026)

Process: two senior advisors (monetary policy; empirical finance) reviewed the paper, exchanged suggestions and prioritized (`reviews/ADVISOR_A.md`, `ADVISOR_B.md`). The author then asked that anything resting on FOMC-window surprises (source-of-move test, policy-vs-remainder split, stance via window news) be left to Note B. The advisors re-prioritized to three questions that need no surprises beyond the weights already used; each was run once by an independent agent with a pre-specified design (code and `RESULT.md` in each folder). Outcome series (PAYEMS, GDPC1, PCEPILFE, USREC, USRECM) are in `data/` (FRED downloads of 11 Nov 2026; not yet in `replication/data/` or `SOURCES.md`). Nothing in `paper/` or `replication/` was changed.

| Folder | Question | Result (all [verified] by the running agent; I have not re-run them) |
|---|---|---|
| `rstar_test/` | Do r*-gap versions of an index predict the economy better than real or nominal versions? | Leans toward r*, not significant after correction. r* has lower out-of-sample error than nominal in 69 of 91 comparisons (80 of 91 vs real); 20 DM rejections (18 for r*) against about 9 by chance; Holm leaves none (smallest p 0.0008). Gains are 2-5% of RMSE, mostly 2008-16 and payrolls 2021-26; nothing for core PCE or recessions (r* slightly worse). NFCI/ANFCI beat the common-basis indexes for unemployment and recessions. |
| `weights/` | Which weights (market, FRB/US, PCA, equal, activity-fitted) say most about the economy ahead? | No alternative beats the market weights (MSE 1.04-1.07 times the market's; 0 of 120 tests significant after adjustment); activity-fitted weights are too imprecise to rank weights (10-year yield share 0.31 [0.00, 0.59], equities 0.69 [0.30, 1.00], dollar 0.00 [0.00, 0.23]). Outcomes cannot settle the weights question. |
| `levelflow/` | Does the level or the 12-month change carry the information, in the mean and the lower tail? | Flows for market, GS and FRB/US (HAC t 3.8-5.9; level 0.0-1.0), in the mean and in the in-sample 10th percentile of payroll growth (tail same as mean); r* basis raises the flow t (market 3.9, 4.3, 5.2 by step; step differences untested). NFCI/ANFCI carry the level. Out-of-sample tail untestable (two recessions). Resolves the paper's tension: the 12-lag Granger result is the movement of the level within the year (sum of lags p=0.17; 11 differences p=2e-7). |

## Assessment (mine; for the author to decide)
- **None of the three gives a decisive new result; each gives a clean, honest answer.** The strongest independent addition is `levelflow/` (resolves a real tension, links to growth-at-risk, shows the NFCI level and rate-based flows carry different things). `rstar_test/` turns "the r* step has no validation" into "small, same-signed, not significant after correction, nothing for core PCE or recessions", which is the honest status of the paper's largest effect. `weights/` is a short identification subsection: the policy-news weights are not beaten, and outcomes cannot rank weights further.
- **Both advisors' organizing question** (which way of measuring financial conditions, relative to what and with which weights, says most about the economy ahead?) is answered by these three: flows not levels (for rate-based indexes); r* basis leans right but unproven; weights cannot be separated. That is a coherent paper, but its answer is "small differences, not significant", not a sharp result. The weights answer in particular overlaps Note B's proportionality material only in motive, not in method.
- **Open for the author:** (1) whether to reorganize Note A around that question (then the agreement material becomes the first exhibit and Sections 5.5 and 6 are replaced by these three results) or keep the present framing and add one short subsection; (2) NFCI/ANFCI's better fit for unemployment and recessions, which the paper currently dismisses as "a different object", deserves a plain statement; (3) the results are not in the package: to use them, port the three folders into `replication/` as steps with macros and claims, add the series to `data/SOURCES.md`, and rerun the sync check.
