# Which weights on the same variables say most about the economy ahead?

**Question.** With the variables (10-year yield, equity valuation, real dollar) and the step fixed, which weights forecast activity best: policy-news (MKT), FRB/US (FRBUS3), first principal component (PC1), equal standardized (EQ) or activity-fitted (ACT)? Also given indexes, and level against change.

**Design (fixed in advance; `01_`-`03_`, `run_all.py`).** Outcomes: unemployment gap, 12-month payroll growth, real GDP growth, core PCE inflation, recession within h months (linear probability). h = 6, 12, 24. Recursive forecasts from 1995-01, own current value as control (as `lib/lp.py`). PC1, EQ and ACT (sign-constrained least squares) are re-estimated at each origin. Primary step r*-gap, secondary real. Primary family: 4 alternatives against MKT, 15 outcome-horizons, 2 forms = 120 paired Diebold-Mariano tests, with Holm and Benjamini-Hochberg adjustment.

**Results (r*-gap; the real step agrees) [verified].**
1. *No alternative beats the market weights.* Change form, MSE relative to MKT (geometric mean of 15 cells): FRBUS3 1.058, PC1 1.042, EQ 1.072, ACT 1.067; MKT is lower in 14, 14, 14 and 12 cells. Raw rejections at 10 percent: 25 of 120 (12 expected by chance); after adjustment, 0. The lead sits in unemployment (DM t 2.3 to 2.8 at every horizon). In levels the sets are indistinguishable (0.98 to 0.99 of MKT) and none beats the no-index forecast (1.06 to 1.09).
2. *Activity-fitted weights are imprecise.* Full-sample shares for unemployment 12 months ahead (change form): 10-year yield 0.31 (90 percent bootstrap interval 0.00 to 0.59), equities 0.69 (0.30 to 1.00), dollar 0.00 (0.00 to 0.23). Market shares (0.30, 0.51, 0.18) and FRB/US shares (0.32, 0.36, 0.32) lie inside. Over 10 outcome-form cells, market shares fall inside all three intervals in 6, FRB/US in 5; mean widths are 0.76, 0.78, 0.61 of a total of 1. The bootstrap distance of fitted shares from MKT (L1 0.55 to 1.4) is two to four times the MKT-FRB/US distance (0.31). Wald tests of proportionality to MKT (FRB/US) reject in 2 (2) of 10, both core PCE. ACT forecasts are the worst of the five. **Outcomes cannot settle the weights.**
3. *Level against change.* For MKT, FRBUS3, PC1, EQ, ACT, GS and FRB/US the change form has the lower MSE (ratio 0.84 to 0.94; significant in 1 to 4 of 15 cells). Levels are worse than no index; changes are about equal to it (PC1, EQ, FRBUS3: 0.97 to 1.00) or better (MKT 0.93, Clark-West significant in 5 of 15). Exception: NFCI and ANFCI levels carry content (0.96 and 0.91 of no-index MSE). They hold credit and leverage variables, so this is not a weights result, but "levels carry no content" does not extend to them.

**Verdict.** Policy-news weights forecast at least as well as any alternative on the same variables, 4 to 7 percent better in the change form (not significant after adjustment). Fitted weights are too noisy to improve on them. Outcomes do not identify the weights.

**Recommendation.** Include one short subsection with one table (MSE ratios to MKT by form and outcome; weight intervals in an appendix), not a new organizing question. It answers the reviewers directly: the market weights are not beaten by model, statistical or fitted weights, and outcomes cannot rank them further. It limits the scope of flows-not-levels (NFCI). Frame it as identification: the index is chosen for its units and interpretation, and outcomes give no reason to change its weights. This builds on Hatzius et al. (2010), who classify weights by source without comparing them. Report all 120 tests; claim no winner. [inference] MKT's lead may reflect its large equity weight (51 percent of index variation against 33 to 36).
