# REVIEW 4: "When Do Financial Conditions Move Like Monetary Policy?" (fci_paper.tex, 41 pp)

Checked against numbers.tex, tab_*.tex, results/18_*, 19_*, both RESULT.md files, LIT_VERIFY.md. Main text is about 26 pp and the appendix about 14 pp.

## Ranked problems

1. **Abstract/Intro/Sec 3.1: "they agree" holds only for persistent paths, and the abstract does not say so.** With a 4-quarter half-life the joint response is 0.83 [0.74, 0.93], which excludes 1 (18_matched_paths; RESULT.md). Sec 3.3 concedes this ("the agreement ... is for persistent paths"). The abstract says "the contrast does not depend on how long the movements last" and never qualifies the agreement. Fix: one clause in the abstract and introduction: "for persistent shifts".

2. **Intro/abstract: "the disagreement is not small" rests on one barely rejected ratio.** The ratio of ratios is 2.49 [1.02, 6.81]. At two quarters the interval includes 1. Table "Two Financial Movements" shows each FRB/US number (0.158 and 0.392) inside the market's own 90% interval ([0.147, 0.351] and [0.081, 0.443]). The market ratio interval [0.33, 2.30] sits just below 2.48. Both RESULT.md files say "barely excludes 1" and "FRB/US weights held fixed, intervals too narrow". The paper states "2.5 times" flatly. Fix: state in the abstract that the contrast is estimated as 2.5 with a 90% interval from 1.0 to 6.8, with the FRB/US weights fixed. Then "disagree" is a fact with its precision, not a headline.

3. **Abstract/Sec 4/Discussion: "no detectable footprint on unemployment" omits the one significant result.** The SPF coefficient on D is -1.29 [-2.38, -0.21] (t = -2.0). Excluding 2008-15 it is -0.46 [-0.89, -0.04]. The 1996+ coefficient is -1.18 [-2.51, 0.14]. All are the opposite sign to the FRB/US reading. Sec 4.4 calls this "suggestive only" and the abstract drops it; the intro mentions the sign only in passing. A significant wrong-signed coefficient is not a null. Either state the SPF result in the abstract as a fact (wrong sign, significant at 10%, 15 unadjusted tests) or say why it does not count.

4. **Sec 4.2: "does not rule out a difference of about half a percentage point in either direction" is wrong.** The main-sample interval is [-1.06, 0.47], so one point downward and half a point upward. On 1996+ it is [-2.33, 0.13]. Fix: give the actual interval. Also say that the test cannot separate the two hypotheses: c = 0 (market right) and c = a = 0.19 (FRB/US right) both lie inside [-0.96, 0.42] (c = a not rejected, t = -1.32; 19_prop p = 0.53 and 0.23). That sentence is the honest core of the null result and is absent. The GDP coefficients (0.89, 1.82, 0.27) are also wrong-signed and are not called so.

5. **Abstract/Discussion/Conclusion: "equity valuation alone carries as much content as either index" is overstated.** In sample the gain is 0.115 against 0.136 for the market index. It equals FRB/US (0.115) only. Out of sample (1976-2026) equities beat the market index (ratio 0.93, DM 2.6), but on 1996+ they do not (ratio 1.018, DM -0.2; 19_oos). Fix: "equity valuation alone raises adjusted R2 by 0.115, against 0.136 for the market index, and forecasts better out of sample on the full sample but not since 1996". Also, FRB/US weights forecast worse than the market weights (ratio 1.07, DM -2.5). That favors the market index and is not in the abstract or Discussion.

6. **Abstract and Conclusion: "it accounts for most of the gap" and "accounts for the equity boom of 1998-2001" are garbled.** The difference IS the gap. What the bridge shows is that equity weights account for 1.29 of 1.73 points (75%), in levels, with a 90% interval of [0.27, 3.03] on the gap. Fix the subject.

7. **"Difference" means two things.** In Sec 2.4, 3.1-3.2, 4 and Fig 1, D is the three-asset, normalized difference in 12-month changes. In Sec 3.4 and the abstract's 1998-2001 claim it is the full FRB/US index (spreads, house prices) less the market index, in levels. Sec 3.4 gives 2022-24 as a 0.96 gap, while Sec 4.5 says D = -0.01 and "the two readings agree" for the same episode. The reader cannot reconcile them. Name the second object (the "index gap"), keep it out of the abstract's sentence on D, and explain the 2022-24 contrast.

8. **Sec 3.1: closeness on announcements is close to mechanical.** D(psi s) = 0 by construction (Sec 2.4). Scaling by a mean market move of 0.053 and "never exceeds a quarter point" is a weak test, since the market move itself never gets near 0.25. The paper omits that |D| exceeds half the move in 25% of announcements [13, 45] (18_D_events share_gt_half_move). Report it, or drop "never exceeds a quarter point".

9. **Sec 3.2: "Someone tracking whether conditions tightened would reach much the same conclusion with either reading" has no supporting statistic.** No sign-agreement rate is reported. The paper shows a ratio of 0.33 and a correlation of -0.61 between D and the market move. Add the share of months with the same sign, or delete the sentence. The 36% share above 0.25 has an interval of [9, 72], which the abstract omits.

10. **Sec 3.4: "The r* and the policy rate contribute almost nothing" is true for levels only.** For 12-month changes the equity r* transformation alone is 0.20 of the variance (RESULT.md). Also, 72/14/49/18/17 in the text is number overload (rule 8: no shares where the intervals are [-1, 91]). The episode level gaps (1.73, 0.96) are centered on 1973-2026 means and in different units; Appendix B itself says the base period moves the level by about a point. The assessment flagged this and Sec 3.4 does not carry the caveat.

11. **Title question is answered by a near-tautology.** "Financial conditions move like policy when they move like policy news" (Discussion, Conclusion) is true by normalization. The non-trivial content is (a) the independent 0.975 agreement, (b) D is a third of the 12-month move, and (c) the composition contrast. Lead with these. Say in one sentence that the market weights are not tested against policy; Boehm-Kroner show equities and the dollar in windows move on a non-yield dimension, which bears directly on psi and is mentioned only as "noise".

12. **Sections 5-8 are a bolted-on second paper.** Sec 5 (nominal/real/r*), 6 (VAR, forward impetus), 7 (wedge, stance test, level vs flow, NFCI, purge, neutral rate, weights) and 8 (1996+, drift, real time) occupy about 12 of 26 pages. The r* step is described in Sec 8.4 as "the largest effect in the paper" and is not part of the question. Sec 8.1 refers to "the sample of Note B". Sec 8.3 reports real-time r* cutting the flow gain, while the primary test in Sec 4 is built on the r* index. That tension is never discussed. Move Sections 5, 6, 7.3-7.6 and 8.2-8.3 to appendices with a half-page summary, or cut. Keep 7.1-7.2 and the NFCI paragraph (one page) as "what both indexes say about unemployment". The abstract's last sentence (NFCI longer horizons) can go. Resulting balance: about 17 pp main text.

13. **Concepts used before definition; forward references.** r*-relative index, "flow", "forward impetus" and "the purge" appear in Sec 1 and 4 (Fig 1 notes, Table 2.1, 4.1, 4.4) and are defined in Sec 5-7. Sec 4.4 begins "The backward-looking purge of Section 7.5 cannot say..." (forward reference doing argument work). Sec 6.1 and 7 refer to Sec 8. Define r* and the flow in Sec 2 or work in nominal changes first.

14. **Persistence evidence is stated as fact.** "The data give no support for short half-lives" rests on AR(1) half-lives of levels and one equity series; RESULT.md tags it [inference] and notes there are no daily 10-year or dollar data. Say what was and was not measured.

15. **Citations.** (a) Intro attributes the non-invariance of MCI weights to eika1996 and batini2002; LIT_VERIFY confirms only Eika et al., while Batini-Turnbull is citation-only and [secondhand] (a model-based dynamic MCI). Drop it from that claim. (b) bletzinger2026 is an ECB Economic Bulletin box; the text says "their finding concerns changes in a macro-weighted index", which is not in LIT_VERIFY, and the publication date is unconfirmed. (c) boehm2025 "so a movement ... can itself come from Fed communication" is an inference; the source says risk premia. (d) sack2023 (Brookings commentary), powell2023/2024 quotes, hatzius2010 Lucas-critique and dudley2010 are not in LIT_VERIFY. Flag them as unverified. (e) The ECB "weaker activity signal at high valuations" was not found and is correctly absent.

## Minor
- 0.97 (Sec 2.3, spsi:market) against 0.975 elsewhere. Use one. The example table's FRB/US column is in unnormalized funds-rate points while the text says both are put on one scale. Say so; the ratio is unaffected.
- Sec 4.5 2004-06: "unemployment fell by more than either weighting implied". Both implied a rise (+0.13, +0.10). Say "fell while both implied a rise". Give the inconvenient 2022-24 episode more than three lines.
- Sec 3.4 "the market index puts more weight on valuations that were far above average" is unsupported. The mean-centering makes it an artifact of the base period.
- Sec 7.5 "to a degree" and "lean toward it without proving it" appear 4 times (Sec 1, 7.5, 8.4, Discussion). This is hedging and repetition; state once.
- Discussion: "a third of the move" is 12-month changes only; say so.
- Fig 1 and Table 'Two Financial Movements': normalized w_B uses 0.975. State it once in the table note.
- Table bridge shows "-0"; "Section 6.1 ... 'the Granger statistic'" is number heavy.

## Summary of what is sound
Units for the worked example reconcile (5 x 0.051 = 0.256; 3.9 x 0.065 = 0.255; ratio 2.48; 0.975). The introduction is clear, and the null and wide interval for composition are stated plainly with intervals. The main fixes are overstatements (items 1-6), the two meanings of "difference", and moving sections 5-8 to the appendix.
