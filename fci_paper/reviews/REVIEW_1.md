# Review 1: against the author's own standards (WRITING_GUIDE sections 1-3, 6)

Checked against numbers.tex, tab_*.tex, indexes_*.csv. The 27-page PDF is 19 pages of main text and 4 of appendix.

## Ranked problems

1. **"Easy becomes tight after 2008" depends on a base period that the text hides.** Abstract, Intro para 4, 3.4, Discussion. The 3.4 figures (1.16 / -0.30 / -0.70) are "with 1990-2026 as zero". Every other figure and the abstract use 1973-2026. On the paper's own base, the 2009-16 averages at the r* step are market -0.22, GS +0.08, FRB/US -0.57 (indexes_rstar.csv). Only the market index moves toward tight, and only on the 1990 base. The sentence "the GS and FRB/US indexes move the same way" is true of the shift, not of the sign. Fix: state the base in the sentence. Say "tighter relative to its 1990-2026 average" and drop "tight" for GS and FRB/US. Alternatively re-run on one base.
2. **"The benchmark is closest to the Goldman Sachs index"** (5.1 bold lead; Conclusion "closest to the index practitioners follow"). It is false at the nominal step: FRB/US 0.98 against GS 0.76 in levels, and since 1990 0.85 against 0.84. Changes are 0.95 for both. At r*, FRB/US 12-month changes are 0.96 against 0.98, a tie in practice. True matched statement: at the real and r* steps the benchmark and GS correlate 0.99 in levels. "Entirely because of the nominal dollar" has no table behind it; since 1990 the nominal correlation is still only 0.84. Delete "entirely".
3. **Agreement with FRB/US falls with each step, but the abstract and 5.1 say agreement rises.** Levels: 0.98, 0.95, 0.94; since 1990: 0.85, 0.78, 0.79. The paper reports only that the range falls. Say so: the steps raise agreement with GS and cut the range, and lower the level correlation with FRB/US.
4. **"The three readings differ by less than the sampling error" (5.4; Intro) is contradicted by the bootstrap.** The FRB/US reading (+0.27) lies above the market index's 90 percent interval (-2.01, 0.24). The bootstrap of the gap (bt:rstar:latest:gap:frbus) runs from -2.28 to -0.03 and excludes zero. By the paper's convention the market and FRB/US readings differ. The comparison "interval width 2.25 against gap 1.5" mixes a width with an error. Rewrite: the GS reading lies inside the interval, the FRB/US reading just outside it.
5. **The weights-robustness rows move the reading by 1.9 points, yet the text says "around the baseline"** (6.3). The range is -1.49 to +0.41, wider than the GS to FRB/US gap and the same size as the bootstrap interval. The ten-year weight ranges from 0.61 to 0.99. Report the spread plainly. It is the same finding as 5.4.
6. **"Most of the difference comes from the weights on equity and the dollar" is shown only for market against FRB/US.** Intro para 4, abstract, Conclusion. GS has a 39.6 percent BBB-spread weight that neither benchmark has. The market-GS gap is 0.3 today, so the four-index range is mostly FRB/US against the others. The "four level indexes" are never defined in the text. Two of them are FRB/US with and without house prices. Say "three indexes", or define the four in the text.
7. **Units are mixed in the headline numbers.** Abstract and Discussion say "about one percentage point of the policy rate". Market and GS are in two-year-yield points and FRB/US in funds-rate points. The range takes max minus min across the two units (Table notes). The scale difference is conceded (sd ratio 1.28) but the abstract does not carry it. The GS conversion error (se 0.15 on 1.04) is not in any interval.
8. **The framing of the question contradicts the Discussion.** Intro: measurement differences are "a matter of definition and can be removed". The r* step is an estimated choice. The Discussion calls it "the largest effect in the paper" and says "no outcome validates it". 5.5 calls the activity regressions "the only evidence". The abstract never answers its own question (how much is measurement, how much weighting). The numbers exist: the 1974-2026 range falls 2.82 to 1.08, but since 1990 only 1.15 to 0.88.
9. **The activity section conflicts with the VAR section.** 5.5 says "levels do not carry information" and the year-ahead reading "responds to what conditions have done". Section 4.1 says the level index Granger-causes unemployment (F 5.3, p<0.0001). "Responds" is causal for a regression association. The FCI-G scale is reported as 0.70 (Table 8) and as 0.47 (Fig 7 and text) with no reconciliation. At the nominal step the GS level has a significant coefficient (0.21, se 0.08), which breaks "levels do not".
10. **The robustness lead sentence is overstated.** "In every row the flow's correlation with FCI-G is at least 0.72" uses point estimates. The VAR-from-1990 row has a 5-95 interval of [-0.03, 0.88], which the sentence hides. 6.2 also does not report that the 4-lag yardstick (0.40) sits at the bottom of the baseline interval (0.39 to 0.83). Add one fact sentence for each.
11. **Discussion: "the 1990-2026 base moves the readings by more than a point."** The movements are 1.38 (market), 1.23 (GS) and 0.92 (FRB/US). The last is below one. Say "about a point".
12. **The "Today" paragraph in 5.3 mixes FRB/US versions.** The text gives FRB/US at +0.27 (no house prices) but explains the gap with the house-price version (+0.16; gap 1.09). The eq and dollar differences (0.84, 0.44) belong to the second version. Also "the closest windows are in the late 1980s and the mid-1990s": Table 6 shows 1986-87, 1989 and 1989-90. There is no mid-1990s window.
13. **Hedging and hand-wringing.** Abstract: "somewhat easier ... somewhat tighter". "One candidate among several, not a standard" appears in the abstract, 5.4 and the Discussion. "Range of about a point" is stated four times. The 12-line "What the results do not show" paragraph holds ten caveats in one block, several already in 6. Guide 2.3 and 2.4 and section 1: state limits once, as facts, in the robustness section. Say the same things once in the abstract and conclusion.
14. **The abstract and Intro lead with level correlations (0.99 / 0.94 / 0.79), but 5.1 says level correlations "overstate agreement" and changes are the better guide.** The abstract has four correlations and "three steps" process language. It says "agree on direction" while citing levels. Lead with the 12-month changes (0.98 / 0.96) and the range.
15. **Terms are inconsistent.** Benchmark, market index and Market (tables). Flow and forward impetus. Yardstick and Phi(12). "Index weighted with FRB/US", "FRB/US index" and "model-weighted index". "Kiley's estimate" while the author is Kiley (Table: "of the author"). Pick one term per concept.
