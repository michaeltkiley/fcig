# Review 3: `fci_paper.tex` (reorganized version)

Checked against `tab_*.tex`, `numbers.tex`, `results/*.csv`, `followup2/3 SUMMARY.md`. Nothing re-run. Ranked by damage.

## Ranked problems

1. **"Not in its level" is stated flatly; the paper's own table breaks it.** Abstract, l.68, Conclusion, Discussion. Sec. 6.1 admits at the end of a paragraph: excluding 2008-15 the level has t = 3.9 (`tab_checks96` row c; followup3: 3.9-4.9). Also in `tab_levelflow` FCI-G (a flow) and the NFCI carry information in levels (t 2.8, 3.8) while its "flow" t is 0.5/1.0. Fix: abstract and intro say "over 1976-2026 the information is in the 12-month change; the level is significant only without 2008-15" once; retitle 6.1 "Flow and Level".
2. **l.68 "as it is in the Board's FCI-G, and not in its level" vs `tab_levelflow`.** FCI-G row: level t 2.8, gain 0.19; 12-month change t 0.5. Sec. 6.1 sentence "FCI-G... carries it in its reading" only makes sense if FCI-G's own value is the flow, but the table then applies "level" and "flow" to FCI-G itself. Define in the table notes what "level" and "flow" mean for FCI-G, or drop the FCI-G comparison from the intro.
3. **"Flow" means two things.** Sec. 4.2 defines flow = forward impetus (VAR-weighted); Secs. 5-6 and every regression use flow = 12-month change (`tab_stance` notes). `tab_activity` is the only table that uses forward impetus. Pick one (use "12-month change" in Secs. 5-6, reserve "flow/forward impetus" for 4.2 and the FCI-G comparison), and fix abstract and conclusion.
4. **2022-24 wedge is stated as a finding; it is not robust to the facts in the paper's own table.** Abstract, l.68, Conclusion, Discussion: "-1.40 points". `tab_episodes_stance`: to December 2023 -0.73 [-1.53, 0.29]; FRB/US-weighted index minus gap is -0.04; with 2-year-news weights the September 2026 index is +0.41 (`tab_robust`). The end-date dependence is in Sec. 5.3 only; the FRB/US and weights dependence is nowhere in text. Also Sec. 5.2: in 2022-26 the index coefficient has the opposite sign (t = -3.3); the abstract leads with that same episode. State once, in the abstract: the 2004-06 wedge is robust (-1.2 to -1.3 for market and GS, -0.73 FRB/US); 2022-24 depends on end date and weights.
5. **Sec. 6.4 "No alternative weights beat the market weights" is wrong as written.** `15_summary.csv`: in the level form the FRB/US, PC1 and equal weights have geometric MSE ratios 0.986, 0.990, 0.978 (13, 10, 12 of 15 cells below 1); in the 12-month change form the GS weights give 0.972 (12 of 15 below 1, 2 better at 10%). The "1.04 to 1.07" is hard coded in the text (not a macro) and covers four sets, change form only. 25 of 120 reject at 10% against 12 expected. Rewrite: "Alternative weights forecast about as well as the market weights; in the change form the four sets are 4-7 percent worse, in the level form 1-2 percent better; none survives Holm." Make the numbers macros.
6. **Sec. 8.1/abstract "holds on 1996-2026, with smaller effects".** `tab_checks96`: Phi(12) is not smaller (0.73 vs 0.65 with 12 lags); the R2 gain is (0.05 vs 0.14); payrolls fail (t -0.8); level t -1.2; the 4-lag row repeats the 12-lag regression columns identically (is it a copy?). The abstract omits payrolls; l.78 includes it. The pre-1990 break is missing: followup2 finds Phi(12) 0.65, 0.39, -0.01 for 1976-89, 1990-2007, 2009-19 and equality rejected at each step; the paper reports 2009-26 (0.55) and 1996-2019 (0.83) instead and never states the 2009-19 value. State the 2009-19 value (and the 3.6-point neutral shift between 1973-89 and 1990-2007 even at r*) or say why not. Followup3 itself says these are unreconciled; the paper should say so.
7. **Sec. 7.1 "agree at every step, a little less at each".** True for 1974-2026 levels (0.98, 0.95, 0.94). False since 1990 (0.85, 0.78, 0.79) and for 12-month changes (0.95, 0.95, 0.96). The sentence "falls slightly with each step" repeats it. Also "agree once both are real" uses 0.99 on full sample, 0.97 since 1990; fine, but cite the since-1990 figure, which is what matters.
8. **Sec. 6.1 tail: "the same as in the mean".** 10th percentile -0.63 [-0.96, -0.08] vs median -0.37; NFCI -1.14. Not the same; say the market flow shifts the tail by 1.7 times the median and the intervals overlap. This is also the only payroll result the paper presents favorably while saying "payrolls fail"; the payroll statements are inconsistent (`tab_stance`: payroll t -2.6, Clark-West 2.0; Discussion "does not carry payrolls on 1996-2026"). Say: payrolls hold 1976-2026, fail 1996-2026.
9. **Sec. 8.2 "The weights drift less than their own sampling error".** Same paragraph: equity weight outside the band at 10 dates, rolling window fails, pseudo-real-time VAR response 0.52 vs 0.65 (followup3). Rewrite as the fact: expanding-window weights stay within the band except equity at 10 dates; response 0.52 vs 0.65.
10. **Purge percentages.** Abstract/intro: "between 45 and 67 percent... is activity that lagged unemployment and inflation explain". The purge is an upper bound (the paper's own caveat says it removes too much, asset prices rightly respond to expected activity). Causal "explain" overstates. Use "falls by 45 to 67 percent under purging". Discussion's "a third to a half of its size" (33-56%) is a different phrasing of the same figure; use one.
11. **Sec. 6.5 "makes the level mean the same thing across regimes to a degree".** `tab_neutrality`: LW leaves +0.88 [0.61, 1.42], excluding zero; only Kiley gets 0.03; the choice of r* reverses the verdict. "Reverses and shrinks" is true only of LW (reverses) and Kiley (zero). Say that.
12. **Structure.** Sec. 7 ("Do the Indexes Agree?") is the old paper's question; after the reorganization it is a second paper in the middle. Keep one table (`tab_compare`), the since-1990 correlations and the range, and move "Where the Indexes Differ" (episodes table, Today figure) and 7.4's bootstrap detail to the appendix. Sec. 2 (existing indexes, FRB/US weights) and Sec. 3 (ladder) come before the question is answered; the stance result arrives on p.~14. Table `tab_stance` is placed after the tests; Sec. 6.2-6.5 (NFCI, purge, neutrality, weights) run as list. Main text is roughly 22 pages; the appendix is thin (6 tables), against the guide's balance.
13. **Overlap with Note B.** The paper builds the index from policy news windows (Sec. 2.2) and states weights and information-effect caveats; that is Note B's territory too. Keep the weights as inputs and cut the "What the weights assume" paragraph to two sentences; point out only the unit, not the identification.
14. **Hedging, repetition, number overload.** "the outcomes lean toward the r* basis without proving it" appears in 6.5, 9 and 8.5 ("lean" three times). The sentence on 69 of 91 / 18 / 2 DM tests is ten counts in one sentence; the paper itself calls them descriptive; one sentence ("69 of 91 below 1; no gain survives Holm") is enough. Sec. 7.4 gives about 20 numbers in two paragraphs. "The claim is about unemployment" appears in 5.2 and 9.
15. **Terms.** "Yardstick" is Phi(12) in 4.1 but "yardstick of the index" and "the yardstick" in 8.x; define once as the 12-month response. "Wedge" is index less funds-rate gap (5.1); the Intro and Sec. 9 also call it "read looser"; "funds-rate gap" is real rate less r*, but the Intro calls it "the real funds rate relative to r*". Keep "funds-rate gap".

- Sec. 8.3 says vintages from 1977, the table starts 1976.
- Sec. 7.1, last sentence of the second paragraph: "Both indexes use the real dollar at every step" contradicts the first paragraph (GS uses the nominal dollar at the nominal step).
- Sec. 5.4 "Equity valuation carries it": add that equities lead the cycle in the same sentence, not a forward reference to 6.4 (guide rule 6).
- "Do not" forward references: l.128 ("Section 4 gives it dynamics"), l.180 ("Section 8 reports four"), l.236. Remove.
- Conclusion's last sentence ("the answer ... is a range, relative to r*") does not parse; end on the 2004-06 wedge and unemployment.
- Title footnote and JEL are fine; abstract is 11 sentences and has results numbers (guide: abstract without results numbers in Note B style; here the wedge numbers could go).
