# Does composition matter for the year-ahead outlook? (step 19; `replication/19_composition.py`)

[verified] = package run (REPS=500, 18 s, deterministic); [inference] = my reading. Units: dM and D in 2-year-yield-equivalent points; D = (w_B - w_M)'dx, w_B = b/(b'psi) with b'psi = 0.975, 12-month changes on the same months as the market-index change [verified: dM equals the step-03 market index change].

**Primary test (one, pre-specified).** Unemployment-gap change at 12 months on own lags, dM and D, 556 origins 1976-2026. Coefficient on D = -0.27, HAC 90% interval [-0.96, 0.42], t = -0.64; bootstrap [-0.89, 0.43]; over weight-estimate draws [-0.60, 0.37]. The adjusted R2 gain from adding D to the market flow is 0.006. Null [verified]. The sign is opposite to the FRB/US reading (c = a would be 0.19), but c = a is not rejected either (t = -1.32). GDP check: 0.89 [-0.77, 2.55], also null and also of the wrong sign for FRB/US.

**Economic units.** Moving D from its 5th to its 95th percentile at the same market-index change (range 1.11, D orthogonal to dM) shifts the year-ahead unemployment-gap change by -0.30 pp, 90% interval [-1.06, +0.47] [verified]. The data cannot exclude half a point either way (sd of the outcome 0.98). 1998-2001 against 2005-06: D differs by 0.29, implied outlook difference -0.08 pp [-0.28, 0.12]; the market-index change differs by -0.12 (-0.02 pp).

**Equities alone (same sample, controls, horizon).** Gain in adjusted R2 over own lags: equities 0.115 (t = -4.45), market flow 0.136 (t = 5.66), three free 0.147, FRB/US-weight flow 0.115, 10-year 0.099, dollar 0.004 (t = 0.62). Equities alone carry most of the content in sample. Out of sample (1995-2025, two recessions) equities alone beat the market flow (RMSE ratio 0.93, Diebold-Mariano t = 2.55) and the FRB/US-weight flow is worse than the market flow (1.07, t = -2.51) [verified, unadjusted]. Free coefficients are not rejected as proportional to w_M (bootstrap p = 0.53) or w_B (0.23); power is low.

**Other samples.** 1996-2026 (318 origins): c = -1.19 [-2.51, 0.14] (market flow alone adds 0.047, equities 0.121); excluding 2008-15 (436): c = +0.32 [-0.13, 0.77] (right sign, null). Clark-West for adding D to the market flow: main -1.79, 1996+ -0.01, ex-2008-15 -1.90 (D worsens forecasts; RMSE ratios 1.10-1.27); GDP 0.70, 1.25, -0.12. No sample shows significant incremental content of D with the FRB/US sign [verified]. The sign is not stable across samples [inference].

**SPF (128 surveys 1990Q3-2025Q3; information month = last complete month before the deadline; monthly averages).** Forecast error in unemployment (realized minus SPF median, four quarters): D coefficient -1.29 [-2.38, -0.21], t = -1.96; D alone -1.17; with the SPF's own expected change -1.19; excluding 2008-15 (88 surveys) -0.46 [-0.89, -0.04]. GDP error: D +2.52 [0.49, 4.54]. A rise in D (FRB/US reads tighter than the market) goes with better-than-forecast outcomes: the opposite of the FRB/US sign. 15 unadjusted coefficients, overlapping outcomes, and 2001 and 2008-09 probably dominate [inference]: suggestive only. The market-index change alone predicts SPF unemployment error (0.17, t = 2.46) but not once D is included.

**Episodes (unemployment, main sample).**
- 1998-2001 (48 origins): mean dM -0.06, D +0.22 (largest of the three). Implied D contribution -0.05 pp [-0.17, 0.07]. Realized change +0.35 pp; recursive forecasts: market +0.67, FRB/US weights +0.78; mean errors -0.32 and -0.43. Market weights closer; difference small.
- 2004-06 (25): dM +0.04, D -0.09; D contribution +0.03. Realized -0.41; both weight sets forecast about +0.1 (errors -0.54, -0.51). Unemployment fell more than either implies.
- 2022-24 (33 origins, 2022-04 onward): dM +1.02 (tightening), D -0.01. The weight sets agree (forecasts +0.65 and +0.66); implied market contribution +0.21 pp [0.09, 0.33]. Realized +0.27; both overpredicted (errors -0.38 and -0.39) while own lags alone erred -0.11 and equities alone -0.20. The index signal worsened the forecast. Agreement between the weight sets is not accuracy [verified].

**Reading (decision rule).** Differences in composition at the 12-month horizon are small relative to the market-index change and leave no stable footprint in unemployment or GDP; intervals are wide. This points to branch (b)/(c): the market index works about as well as the macro-weight index, equities carry much of the content, and the labor-market question merits the lead [inference].

## Addendum (12 Nov 2026): direct tests of the two restrictions, and the SPF decomposition

[verified] = package run (REPS=500, about 28 s, deterministic; existing step-19 outputs byte-identical). Tables: `results/19_restrictions.csv`, `results/19_spf_decomp.csv`; scalars `comp_restr_*`, `comp_spfd_*` (file `scalars_19_composition_ext.csv`).

**1. The restrictions.** The regression is y = ... + beta dM + gamma D, with D = dB - dM computed once on the same 12-month changes, so the FRB/US reading is dB = dM + D. The market index alone implies gamma = 0. The FRB/US index alone implies y = k (dM + D), so beta = gamma, that is gamma - beta = 0. A test of gamma = 0 rejects the market reading if it fails; a test of gamma - beta = 0 rejects the FRB/US reading if it fails. beta and gamma are the `a_dM` and `c_D` of Table comp (same regression, same sample).

Unemployment, main sample, no funds-rate gap: beta 0.19 [0.05, 0.33]; gamma -0.27 [-0.96, 0.42] (bootstrap [-0.89, 0.43]); gamma - beta -0.46 [-1.03, 0.11] (bootstrap [-0.95, 0.13]). With the funds-rate gap: gamma -0.28 [-0.93, 0.37]; gamma - beta -0.45 [-1.03, 0.12]. Excluding 2008-15: gamma 0.32 [-0.13, 0.77]; gamma - beta 0.03 [-0.31, 0.37]. 1996+: gamma -1.18 [-2.51, 0.14]; gamma - beta -1.20 [-2.33, -0.06] (with the funds-rate gap -1.26 [-2.36, -0.15]). GDP, main: gamma 0.89 [-0.77, 2.55]; gamma - beta 1.05 [-0.38, 2.47]; no GDP block rejects either restriction.

Verdict. Gamma = 0 (market reading sufficient) is not rejected in any of 12 blocks, by HAC or bootstrap. Gamma = beta (FRB/US reading sufficient) is rejected in 2 of 12 blocks by HAC at 10 percent (unemployment, 1996+, without and with the funds-rate gap) and in 1 of 12 by the bootstrap (1996+, no funds-rate gap); it is not rejected in the main sample, where the point estimate (-0.46) is large against beta (0.19) and the interval just includes zero (upper end 0.11), nor excluding 2008-15, where gamma = beta holds almost exactly (0.03). Neither restriction is rejected in the main sample, so the data do not choose; where one is rejected it is the FRB/US one [verified]. These are 24 unadjusted tests with overlapping samples; two rejections in 12 is about what chance gives at 10 percent [inference].

**2. SPF decomposition** (128 surveys 1990Q3-2025Q3, 88 excluding 2008-15; same information dates as the SPF test; regressors dM and D; HAC 4, 8-survey blocks). Forecast = SPF median four quarters ahead minus the last observed rate (UNEMP1, the previous-quarter value); realized = realized rate minus the same base; error = realized minus forecast. The coefficients on the forecast and the error add to the coefficient on the realized change in every case (largest discrepancy 9e-16; bootstrap draws 4e-15) [verified]. Coefficient on D (units: pp of unemployment per point of D; effect = D from its 5th to 95th percentile at a given dM, range 0.90 all, 0.84 ex 2008-15; HAC 90% interval):

GDP (same source; forecast growth RGDP6/RGDP2, no "last observed" base; realized is current-vintage growth over the same quarters). Coefficient on D: forecast -0.35 [-0.93, 0.23], realized 2.17 [-0.05, 4.38], error 2.52 [0.49, 4.54]; effect of the error 2.27 pp [0.44, 4.09]. Excluding 2008-15: forecast -0.87 [-1.63, -0.12], realized 0.75 [-0.58, 2.08], error 1.62 [0.32, 2.92] (bootstrap [-0.50, 3.03]).

**Economic reading** [inference]. When FRB/US reads the same asset movements as tighter than the market index does (D positive), unemployment tends to come in below the survey forecast, and GDP above it; that is the opposite of what the FRB/US reading implies. Over all surveys D mainly predicts what subsequently happened, not what forecasters expected: the forecast coefficient on D is small and not different from zero (-0.18), the realized change carries -1.47, and the error accounts for about 88 percent of it. Moving D from its 5th to 95th percentile lowers the unemployment error by 1.16 pp [0.19, 2.14]. Excluding 2008-15 the pattern is weaker and the forecasters lean the FRB/US way (forecast +0.29, null), while the outcome does not follow: the error is -0.39 pp [-0.75, -0.04] (HAC; the bootstrap interval includes zero), realized change null. The all-survey result is probably dominated by the 2008-09 and 2001 episodes (it weakens when 2008-15 is excluded; not checked survey by survey); 12 of the 24 new coefficients are significant at 10 percent by HAC, among unadjusted, overlapping, small-sample estimates. Reading: the data give no support for D carrying extra tightening of the FRB/US sign, and the survey forecasters do not appear to use it.
