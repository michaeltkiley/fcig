Equity: consumption and investment (C + residential + BFI) are 120 percent of the GDP effect; net exports are +0.0231 (offset, -33%); the effect is a domestic-demand effect, mostly consumption (wealth and cost of capital). Dollar: net exports are -0.1666 (94% of the GDP effect); domestic demand is 0.6 percent. Half-life 4 quarters: equity -0.0599 (0.130 points; consumption 91%, BFI 28%); dollar -0.1531 (0.332 points; net exports 91%).
Hypotheses: consumption and investment account for the equity result [yes]; the dollar result operates chiefly through net exports [yes, exports 73 percent; imports a further 21 percent].
Hike (+1 pp, full transmission), quarter 4: GDP -0.4616; ten-year +0.37 pp, stock wealth -13.9 percent, dollar +1.56 percent, mortgage spread -0.01, BBB spread +0.05. Components: consumption -0.307, BFI -0.109, residential -0.064, inventories -0.059, exports -0.048, imports +0.118, government +0.007.
Replay of the usual announcement response (psi, persistent, current vintage): 0.954 funds-rate points (stored: 0.975); GDP shares: ten-year 32%, equity 25%, dollar 44%. The hike's own quarter-4 spillovers priced the same way: ten-year 21%, equity 42%, dollar 15%, BBB 1% of the hike's GDP effect, 21% remainder (policy-rate and other channels). Asset moves per unit: hike/psi = 0.69 (ten-year), 1.78 (equity), 0.37 (dollar). So the model's own policy transmission is equity-heavy and dollar-light compared with announcement responses; the near-one reading of the usual response comes from offsetting compositions.
