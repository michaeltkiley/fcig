# The author's assessment of the stance paper and the new direction (12 Nov 2026), condensed faithfully

**Verdict.** The FCI paper is a useful measurement paper ranked below Transmission (Note B). Its headline (financial conditions add information about unemployment beyond the funds-rate gap) survives demanding checks, but the Goldman Sachs replication delivers essentially the same result (adjusted R2 gain 0.12 GS, 0.10 FRB/US, 0.11 market), so a reader learns little about the new index. Keep most of the empirical work, change the organizing question.

**New organizing question: "When do movements in financial conditions move like monetary policy?"** Two independent ways to answer: (i) start with a policy surprise and measure how asset prices move (event study; market weights w_M, normalized w_M'psi = 1); (ii) start with asset-price movements and measure how spending responds (FRB/US weights b_h for a stated outcome, horizon and asset-price path). They agree almost exactly on the combination of asset prices that typically moves around an FOMC announcement (market 1.00, FRB/US 0.97 in Table 3) and materially disagree on some combinations that occur in the economy. How often does the difference matter, what drives it, does it change the assessment of the outlook?

**Worked example from Table 3 (rounded weights; market in 2-year-yield points, FRB/US in funds-rate points):** equity valuation falls 5 log points: market +0.255, FRB/US +0.160; dollar appreciates 3.9 log points: market +0.254, FRB/US +0.390; typical joint response to a one-unit surprise: 1.00 and 0.97. The two movements look identical through the market index; FRB/US weighs the dollar move about 2.4 times the equity move (4-quarter GDP effect, persistent-shift experiment). The trap: a persistent dollar appreciation in FRB/US and the dollar's impact response to policy news are different experiments; disagreement may reflect composition or assumed persistence.

**Empirical work, in this order.**
1. *What agreement on announcements tells us.* psi = three asset-price responses to conventional policy news; w_M market weights (w_M'psi = 1); b_h FRB/US weights for a stated outcome/horizon/path. Report b_h'psi in original units first (preserves the 0.97); then w_{B,h} = b_h / (b_h'psi) for common-scale comparison. For financial movements x: D_h(x) = (w_{B,h} - w_M)'x; D_h(psi s) = 0 for any conventional surprise s. Agreement needs the weight difference to have little exposure to the combinations of asset prices that occur. Appendix: VAR/MA version and conditions under which market weights recover spending weights up to scale. The criteria differ: event weights favor assets that recover policy news precisely; macro weights favor assets with larger spending effects.
2. *Does disagreement survive a fair comparison?* Start with exactly the three common assets, transformations, dates, centering and units. Vary only the weights; then add the contributions of policy rates, spreads and housing; then the r* and equity transformations: an accounting of why readings differ, and whether the discrepancy attributed to equities survives a like-for-like comparison. FRB/US exercise: price the same initial financial movements under the persistent paths now used and under a common set of empirically plausible decaying paths; keep paths fixed across weight comparisons; show results at two and four quarters. If the equity-dollar contrast largely disappears with matched paths, that is itself a finding (different assumptions about persistence, not about asset classes). First empirical figure: the distribution of D_h for announcement windows (preferably held-out events) and for ordinary financial movements, absolute size and size relative to total financial movement; for the monthly history: typical absolute difference, frequency of differences above an economically interpretable threshold, and their duration. Bootstrap the shared event estimates jointly.
3. *Does composition matter for the outlook?* One primary test: year-ahead unemployment on the market-index change alongside the matched, normalized FRB/US-minus-market difference computed on the same changes. Four-quarter GDP growth as the model-facing check. Alongside: (a) equities alone on exactly the same sample and controls (Section 5.4 reports gain 0.13 for equities vs 0.11 for the market flow); (b) SPF: match the financial observations to the Philadelphia Fed SPF deadline and ask whether composition predicts the subsequent unemployment forecast error (incremental information test); (c) precision in economic units: what the interval implies for the difference in the year-ahead outlook across two historically observed asset combinations. Episodes: 1998-2001 (largest sustained post-1990 disagreement, mostly equities), 2004-06 (why broad prices and the funds rate diverge), 2022-24 (harder case: GS and market wedges about -1.5, FRB/US wedge near zero, end-date dependent, opposite-sign subsequent relationship); give the inconvenient episode as much prominence as the successful one. ECB's separate finding: US macro-driven FCI movements have a weaker activity signal at elevated equity valuations (competing interpretation).

**Points about the current draft to fix:** the wedge LEVEL claim is weakly supported (units differ, centered on means; base period moves the September 2026 market reading from -0.93 to +0.45; Table 5 validates changes, the 1.29 is an average level difference); abstract and introduction describe the 2008-15 exclusion oppositely (Table C.1: the level becomes significant when those years are excluded).

**Literature that changes the choice (to be verified, citations unverified):** Bletzinger, Martorana and Mistak (ECB, Sept 2026): US Macro-Finance FCI in policy-rate-comparable units, compared with GS and FCI-G, equity-driven divergences, 2022-24 strongest tightening in their sample; Caballero, Caravello and Simsek (FCI*: high bar for neutral financial conditions; our r* adjustment is different; vintage r* from 2006 cuts the flow gain from 0.065 to 0.019, so keep r* as an explanation for historical readings, not the lead); Hatzius et al. (FCI conditional on past activity and inflation), Aramonte-Rosen-Schindler (rankings depend on the crisis), Lombardi-Manea-Schrimpf (safe yields vs priced financial risk; risk has stronger predictive content for activity); Arnaut-Bauer, Ajello et al. (model-based multipliers), the older monetary conditions index literature (invariant policy meaning of asset-price movements); Boehm-Kroner (FOMC announcements move equities and exchange rates along a dimension orthogonal to yield changes).

**Working title:** "When Do Financial Conditions Move Like Monetary Policy?"

**Decision rule.** Run the matched three-asset comparison and the single composition regression BEFORE rewriting the introduction. (a) If differences survive common units and plausible asset paths, recur outside one equity boom, and have discernible implications for GDP or unemployment: reorganize the paper around them. (b) If the indexes are effectively interchangeable for ordinary movements and the exceptions can be bounded: a persuasive paper on why a simple market index works about as well as a macro-model index and where its interpretation ends. (c) If outcome intervals are wide and most apparent disagreement comes from centering or unmatched inputs: move to the labor-market question (market flow vs NFCI level by horizon, against equities alone, a credit-spread benchmark and the contemporaneous forecast) and give that result the lead. Third (least preferred) option: the wedge vs funds-rate gap measured against a maturity-matched real two-year rate and a broader proxy funds rate, with comparison to FCI*.
