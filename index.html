Replicating the Fed's FCI‑G from public data

The Federal Reserve Board's FCI‑G measures how changes in interest rates, stock and house prices, and the dollar push on real GDP growth over the next year. The Fed publishes it monthly; this page rebuilds it every weekday from public data, using the Fed's published weights and formula, so it can be read daily and compared with any date since 2020, such as the day before an FOMC meeting. Units are percentage points of real GDP growth over the next year. Positive values are a headwind; negative values are a tailwind.

Method and data

The index follows the Technical Appendix of Ajello, Cavallo, Favara, Peterman, Schindler and Sinha (2023), "A New Index to Measure U.S. Financial Conditions," FEDS Notes. For each variable, three‑month changes over the last 12 quarters (3‑year lookback) or 4 quarters (1‑year) are multiplied by the Fed's published weights (Table 1, from the FRB/US model and other Board models) and summed.

  • Rates (fed funds, 10‑year, mortgage, BBB): change in the three‑month average of daily values.
  • Stocks and house prices: 100 × three‑month log change in the level.
  • Dollar: 100 × log change in the three‑month average.

Sources. FRED: effective fed funds (DFF), 10‑year Treasury (DGS10), Freddie Mac 30‑year mortgage rate (MORTGAGE30US), ICE BofA BBB effective yield (BAMLC0A4CBBBEY), Moody's Baa (DBAA), broad dollar (DTWEXBGS, TWEXB, TWEXBMTH), Case‑Shiller national (CSUSHPISA). Yahoo Finance: Dow Jones U.S. Total Stock Market Index (^DWCF), with the S&P 500 before 1995 and FRED's SP500 for days not yet available. Zillow: ZHVI, United States, mid‑tier, smoothed and seasonally adjusted. Fed: published FCI‑G for validation.

Where it differs from the Fed.

  • BBB yield before Oct 2023: FRED carries only a rolling three years of ICE data, so this page stores the ICE history it has collected and uses changes in Moody's Baa for earlier dates. This matters for history, not for the latest values: the 1‑year BBB component matches to within 0.003.
  • Mortgage rate: the weekly Freddie Mac survey tracks the Fed's series closely but not exactly (error up to about 0.05), so the Fed likely uses a different or daily mortgage rate.
  • Stocks: at a few month‑ends the Fed's index appears to use the previous day's close, for example Feb 27 instead of Feb 28, 2025. This explains most of the remaining stock error.
  • Pre‑1995 stocks and pre‑2000 house prices are spliced from the S&P 500 and Case‑Shiller.
  • Latest days: Zillow publishes monthly, so its latest level is carried forward until the next release. When the Dow Jones Total Market index is not yet available, S&P 500 changes fill in and are replaced when it arrives. The footer lists the latest date for each input.
